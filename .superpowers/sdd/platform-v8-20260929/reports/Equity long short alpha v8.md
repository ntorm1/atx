# Longer history and slower trading lift v8

The 2026 literature says the v8 book gains most from three unglamorous moves: lengthen the sample that judges it, slow both the signal and the trade, and add a small number of signals built from information the book does not yet use. No missing signal family earns grade-A evidence once it is tested after 2004 outside microcaps, and the researchers' own estimate for ten to twenty new slow members is **+0.05 to +0.15 net Sharpe**, which is their arithmetic and not a published number. Newer evidence reverses three v6 recommendations: the latest announcement return as the core of earnings momentum, cash-based profitability as the quality upgrade, and multi-lag seasonality. The two construction experiments that v7 recommended and TRAIN rejected (cost-shrunk targets and an alpha-maximising optimiser) failed for reasons the literature predicts, and the supported replacements are a target-tracking optimiser, rank hysteresis, horizon-weighted aims and trade rates re-tuned per asset level, all judged at $4-8bn rather than $1bn. The owner's expansion of TRAIN from 2020-2022 to 2020-2023 cuts every Sharpe standard error by only 13%, so single +0.08 steps remain undetectable and improvements must be tested as pre-registered bundles; the possible 2013-2019 backward extension would cut those errors by nearly half relative to the three-year window (about 40% relative to four years) and is the largest statistical lever available, but it needs engineering work and an owner decision. The hidden block is not uniform: 2024 was read twice at book level and can now falsify but not certify, while 2025 onward has never been read and is the only pristine evidence left. Automated alpha discovery is cheap to generate and unreliable to judge, so the platform should automate evaluation, trial accounting and marginal-contribution scoring before it builds any miner, and hold mined candidates to hurdles of t 3.5 to 4.6. The researchers' planning value for live net Sharpe is **0.7 to 1.0** against the 1.405 measured on 2020-2022, and that too is an estimate.

A note on evidence quality applies to the whole report. All five research notes record that the web-search budget ran out before every source could be checked, so items the notes list as gaps are unresearched, not rejected. Three labels are preserved from the notes throughout: **[est]** marks a researcher's own arithmetic or judgement (roughly plus or minus 50%), **CZ-own** marks a researcher's own computation on the public Chen-Zimmermann portfolio files (gross of costs, monthly rebalanced, CRSP universe, never a published number), and **secondary** or **snippet** marks a figure taken from a summary rather than the primary text. Where this report extends the notes' formulas to the new four-year TRAIN window, the result is marked **[report arithmetic]**. Book facts (net Sharpe 1.405, gross 1.809, volatility 4.13%, turnover 3.8% of gross per day, 13.5 bps per traded dollar, gross leverage 1.247, about 1,850 names, deflated Sharpe 0.82 at 37 trials) come from the sprint brief as quoted in the notes, and data-availability statements come from the local atx-db documents (`C:/atx/atx-db/docs/ALPHA_PANEL*.md`).

## Four years of TRAIN cuts standard errors 13%; the backward extension cuts them nearly in half

The binding constraint on every question in this report is statistical power, so the data budget comes first. Under independent returns the Sharpe estimator has variance (1 + SR^2/2)/T ([Lo 2002](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2469/faj.v58.n4.2453)), and the non-normal generalisation used for the probabilistic Sharpe ratio and minimum track-record length adds skewness and kurtosis terms ([Bailey-Lopez de Prado 2012](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1821643)). For a paired difference between two highly correlated books the researchers derive a standard error of about sqrt(2(1 - rho)/Y) in annual units, and they note that this formula reproduces the platform's observed paired standard errors of 0.09-0.12 on three years at rho 0.98-0.99 [est]. Applying the same formulas to the owner's new window gives the table below. The three-year and eleven-year columns are the researchers' estimates; the four-year column is this report's extension of their formulas.

|---|---|---|---|
| SE of annual Sharpe (normal returns) | 0.58 | 0.50 | 0.30-0.32 |
| SE of paired Sharpe difference, rho 0.98 | 0.116 | 0.100 | 0.061-0.064 |
| SE of paired Sharpe difference, rho 0.99 | 0.082 | 0.071 | 0.043 |

The message of the four-year column is that **the TRAIN expansion helps but changes no decision rule**. A true +0.08 improvement has a t-statistic of 0.8 on four years, and the researchers compute that accepting such steps one at a time needs 17 to 25 years at rho 0.98 [est]. The practical consequence is their bundle rule: register improvements as a bundle, accept the bundle on its cumulative paired difference against the frozen baseline, and require each member only to have a non-negative difference and the sign the literature predicts. Their worked example is five steps totalling +0.40 at correlation 0.93 to the baseline, which gives t 1.85 on three years and 3.5 on eleven [est]; the same bundle gives roughly t 2.1 on four years [report arithmetic]. Paired tests should use the studentized circular block bootstrap, because the Jobson-Korkie-Memmel test "is not robust against fat tails or time series effects, where it becomes liberal" and heteroskedasticity-consistent inference "is often liberal in finite samples" ([Ledoit-Wolf 2008](https://www.econ.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:ffffffff-935a-b0d6-0000-00007214c2bc/jef_2008pdf.pdf)). A 2025 paper gives a closed-form Sharpe sampling distribution under joint non-normality and serial correlation with public code, but the researchers could not retrieve its full text, so its exact formula is not reproduced here ([Lopez de Prado-Lipton-Zoonekynd 2025](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5520741)).

The deflated Sharpe gate ([Bailey-Lopez de Prado 2014](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551)) illustrates the same point. Backing out from the book's own figures (Sharpe 1.405, three years, 37 trials, deflated Sharpe 0.82), the researchers infer a null benchmark near 0.84-0.88 and estimate that the 0.95 gate needed a Sharpe of about 1.85 on three years, against roughly 1.0 to 1.4 on eleven years, depending on how much of the dispersion across trials is real design difference and how much is noise [est]. On four years with the cross-trial variance carried over, an unchanged Sharpe of 1.405 gives a deflated Sharpe of about 0.85 and the gate needs roughly 1.7 [report arithmetic]. The researchers stress that the cross-trial variance must be re-estimated on the new trial series and not carried over, so these figures are indicative only.

### The hidden block has two grades, and the notes' proposal differs from the owner's decision

The validation note proposed folding all of 2023-2024 into development and reserving 2025 onward as a single-shot gate. The owner's decision is narrower: 2023 joins TRAIN, and 2024 onward stays hidden. This report follows the owner's decision and keeps the researchers' warning about what the two earlier reads cost. Selecting among k looks inflates the expected best result by 0.56 standard errors for two looks; on the two-year 2023-2024 block the researchers estimate that two adaptive reads carry an optimistic bias of **up to about 0.40 Sharpe in level or 0.08 in a paired difference**, the same size as the improvements being accepted [est]. Two consequences follow. First, 2023 enters TRAIN already partly fitted: design choices that survived the two validation reads were selected with 2023 in view, so TRAIN statistics on 2023 flatter the incumbent. Second, 2024 stays hidden but is not clean; on one year the standard error of a Sharpe ratio is about 1.0 and of a paired difference about 0.20 at rho 0.98 [report arithmetic], so 2024 can reject a broken book and cannot confirm a +0.08 step. Only 2025 onward has never been read. With about 1.7 years it has a Sharpe standard error near 0.77 and "can falsify (for example SR < 0) but cannot confirm SR >= 1" [est].

The protocol literature is blunt about reuse. "Suppose a model is successful in the in-sample period but fails out of sample... The researcher modifies the initial model so it then works both in sample and out of sample. This is no longer an out-of-sample test. It is overfitting," and "the training sample needs to be justified in advance. The sample should never change after the research begins" ([Arnott-Harvey-Markowitz 2019](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P138_A_backtesting_protocol.pdf)). The TRAIN change is therefore itself an event to log and justify in the trial ledger before any v8 result is read. For the hidden block the researchers adapt the reusable-holdout idea: the holdout answers only pass or fail against pre-registered thresholds, with a fixed budget of failures, and no holdout return series or attribution is shown to researchers ([Dwork et al. 2015](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/file/bad5f33780c42f2588878a9d07405083-Paper.pdf)). They caution that the formal guarantee assumes independent samples and bounded queries, which Sharpe ratios on autocorrelated heavy-tailed returns do not satisfy, so the protocol is a heuristic that borrows the logic. Under the owner's decision the natural form is a gate job that reports 2024 and 2025-onward as separate pass/fail bits, with 2025 onward as the only certifying segment.

### The backward extension is the most valuable unused asset, and it is not free

The researchers call the never-used pre-2020 block "the most valuable asset" and recommend running the frozen v7 book on it once, pre-registered, before any tuning, because it is the only place where the accepted +0.07 to +0.09 improvements can be re-tested on data that did not select them. Prices start in March 2012, so any 252-session lookback first exists about March 2013, and the full book exists from about April 2013; the researchers' usable sample of 2013-04 to 2022-12 is about 9.75 years, and adding 2023 makes it about 10.75 years [report arithmetic]. They attach four conditions. The runner must be validated by byte-identical reproduction of the existing 2020-2022 result, never by looking at returns on the new block. The expectation must be pre-registered that Sharpe on 2013-2019 is lower than on 2020-2022, because that is decay in the direction the literature predicts and not a bug. Inference is valid only if the 2012-2019 universe includes delisted names and fundamentals are as first reported, which no source quantifies and the platform must measure itself. And any recency weighting must be fixed in advance, because forecasts are highly sensitive to the down-weighting parameter ([Pesaran-Pick-Pranovich 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304407613000687)); their suggestion is equal weights for inference and a five-year half-life only for fitted quantities. A Bayesian illustration shows what is at stake: with an assumed prior of 0.7 (sd 0.4) for a diversified net book, a sample Sharpe of 1.405 yields a posterior near 0.92 on three years and near 1.14 on eleven, so the extension is "worth about +.2 of credible Sharpe even if the point estimate does not change" [est; the prior is an assumption of the researchers, not a cited figure]. Until the owner decides, every v8 experiment below is sized for four years of TRAIN, and the experiments that only become testable on ten years are listed as deferred.

## No missing family earns grade A, and information type decides orthogonality

The two large replication projects set the base rate. With NYSE breakpoints and value weights, **286 of 447 anomalies (64%) are insignificant at the 5% level** and 85% fail t >= 3, and the worst category is trading frictions, where 95 of 102 variables fail, including Amihud illiquidity, share turnover and idiosyncratic volatility ([Hou-Xue-Zhang, NBER w23394](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)). The opposing project classifies 153 factors into 13 themes, finds a US replication rate of 56.9% on raw returns against Hou-Xue-Zhang's 35%, and estimates replication above 75% in ten of the thirteen themes, with seasonality, leverage and size the exceptions ([Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen, NBER w28432](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). A third result removes any comfort from peer review: mining 29,000 accounting ratios for t > 2 gives post-sample predictability similar to published predictors, and about half of predictability survives the original sample for both ([Chen-Lopez-Lira-Zimmermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317)). The most sobering recent number is a working paper, not yet peer-reviewed: across about 200 published anomalies the median return in the top-90%-of-capitalisation universe (about 3,000 stocks) is 26 bps per month through 2005 and **7 bps per month after 2005**, with "a handful of profitability and financing signals" as the exceptions ([Chen-Welch 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06502)).

Against that base rate the researcher screened every family the platform could build, using a uniform test: long-short returns over 2005-2024 from the Chen-Zimmermann portfolio files, ex-microcap and value-weighted, plus a spanning regression on nine theme proxies built from the same files ([openassetpricing.com data](https://www.openassetpricing.com/data/); definitions from [SignalDoc.csv](https://github.com/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection/blob/master/SignalDoc.csv)). Every number in the next table is CZ-own. The researcher's caveats are material: the portfolios are gross, monthly and on the CRSP universe, so they overstate what a top-3000, cost-aware, slow book earns; the theme proxies are not the library itself and include no options or ownership-flow proxy; and for Tax, Coskewness, G-score, EarningsConsistency and ExchSwitch no independent published post-2004 large-cap test was found, so these computations are the only post-2004 evidence and come from one data source. The researcher's verdict is that **grade A is empty** and the highest honest grade for a new, buildable, slow signal is B.

### Famous families fail the post-2004 test, and the strongest results need data the platform lacks

The same screen rejects more than it admits. In the researcher's CZ-own computations for 2005-2024, Amihud illiquidity earns -.09% per month ex-microcap (t -.65), share turnover -.04 (t -.27), long-term reversal -.03 (t -.11), Soliman's asset-turnover change .04 (t .51), hiring .21 ex-microcap and -.24 value-weighted, net debt financing -.33 value-weighted (t -1.85), dividend initiation .12 (t .75) and price delay -.52 (t -2.93, wrong sign) ([own computation from Chen-Zimmermann files](https://www.openassetpricing.com/data/)). Net external financing still earns .61 (t 2.43) ex-microcap but its spanning alpha is -.03 (t -.24) with R2 .81, and the repurchase indicator has spanning alpha .01 with R2 .71, so both are the existing book under another name. Same-calendar-month seasonality is the sharpest negative: the lag-1 signal earns -.23 (t -1.13), lags 2-5 earn -.03 ex-microcap and **-.65 value-weighted (t -2.06)**, and only lags 16-20 keep a premium (.45, t 3.28), which cannot be built from prices that begin in March 2012. The existing single-lag seasonality member therefore deserves a review, not an extension.

The strongest published cross-firm results are data-gated. Shared-analyst-coverage momentum has a value-weighted five-factor alpha of 1.19% per month (t 6.71) and subsumes industry, geographic, customer and technology momentum, but it needs analyst coverage data and its longer lags are insignificant among large stocks ([Ali-Hirshleifer 2020](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/AQR-Insight-Award/2019/Shared-Analyst-Coverage_Ali-Hirshleifer.pdf)). "Lazy Prices" earns 34-58 bps per month value-weighted for up to 18 months with modest turnover, and needs EDGAR text ingestion ([Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen, NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)). Technological links need patents, with a value-weighted spread of 69 bps per month reported in a secondary summary ([Alpha Architect on Lee-Sun-Wang-Zhang 2019](https://alphaarchitect.com/technological-links-and-predictable-returns/)). Customer-supplier and conglomerate signals need segment data that the platform's XBRL Company Facts source drops. The buildable substitute, Hou's industry lead-lag, earned .55 (t 2.43) ex-microcap over 2005-2024 but only .06 (t .24) value-weighted over 2012-2024, loads -.54 on reversal and is a one-month signal, and a critique attributes longer-horizon cross-firm predictability to commonality in momentum alone ([Burt-Hrdlicka 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/where-does-the-predictability-from-sorting-on-returns-of-economically-linked-firms-come-from/6B9F2560E0A27615572309C6F295AFBF)). The connected-stocks strategy through common fund ownership earned a five-factor alpha of 76 bps per month (t 4.96) among stocks above the NYSE median, but on 1980-2008 data for active mutual funds, and 13F on the platform carries no filer type ([Anton-Polk 2014](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/ConnectedStocks.pdf)).

### The N-th signal pays only when its correlation with the book is far below the book's own average

Orthogonality follows the information source and not the label. In the researcher's spanning regressions, signals built from the same income-statement and balance-sheet items are 50-80% explained by the existing themes (XFIN .81, repurchases .71, five-year share growth .63, percent accruals .48), while signals built from co-moments, tax accounts, listing events, filing events and the open price are 10-35% explained (ExchSwitch .11, coskewness .20, Tax .25, AgeIPO .26) ([own computation](https://www.openassetpricing.com/data/)). The researcher's ranking by likely orthogonality puts filing events first, then coskewness, tax-to-book income, the overnight-intraday split, long-lag seasonality (not buildable), 13F structure, and cross-firm momentum last; correlations with the library's options, insider, 13F and short-volume members could not be measured because the Chen-Zimmermann files have no equivalent portfolios. The theme proxies also show that the existing book is less diversified than its labels suggest: earnings momentum and price momentum correlate .73 and profitability and short interest .60, with an average pairwise correlation of .113.

The arithmetic of the marginal signal is unforgiving. For N signals with equal Sharpe s and equal correlation rho the equal-risk combination earns s x sqrt(N / (1 + (N-1) rho)), with a ceiling of s / sqrt(rho); at the measured .113 and s = 0.4 the ceiling is 1.19, nine themes already reach 0.87, and themes ten to twelve add about 0.05 [est]. The literature agrees on "many signals, few themes": a small number of principal components approximates the pricing kernel while no short list of characteristics does ([Kozak-Nagel-Santosh, NBER w24070](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24070/w24070.pdf)), the kernel is "dense in the space of observable factors" ([Bryzgalova-Huang-Julliard 2023](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2743/1/BRYZGALOVA%20-%20Bayesian%20Solutions%20for%20the%20Factor%20Zoo.pdf)), and transaction costs raise the number of jointly significant characteristics from 6 to 15 because rebalancing trades cancel ([DeMiguel et al. 2020](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/1124/1/DeMiguel_TransactionCostPerspective.pdf)). About 15 factors span 153 US factors ([Swade-Hanauer-Lohre-Blitz 2024](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4605976)), which bounds the independent bets available from public price and accounting data. In the researcher's gross proxy exercise an unselected pool of 45 buildable candidates had an average pairwise correlation of .02 and lifted a nine-theme proxy book from a Sharpe of .58 to .81 at a 20% pool weight and .95 at one-third (ex-microcap, 2005-2024), yet only 13-38% of pool members were individually significant and the pool correlated .46-.72 with the book (CZ-own). After the post-publication haircut, costs and the restriction to slow signals, the researcher's expectation is **+0.05 to +0.15 net Sharpe from 10-20 new members** [est], in line with v6's +8% gross from three new independent themes.

The owner's TRAIN decision shapes admission more than selection. Five-year lookbacks (`comp_eq_iss_5y`, `coskew_60m`) exist from 2017 and so cover all of 2020-2023; off-season seasonality at lags 6-10 has its full window only from 2022 and covers half of TRAIN; 13F aggregates begin in 2013q2. With candidate t-statistics of 1.5-3 after 2004, the researcher expects perhaps a third of 10-20 pre-registered candidates to pass an admission test on a short TRAIN by skill and several by luck, and every pre-registered candidate, including those rejected, counts toward the effective number of trials [est]. A four-year TRAIN gives a standalone sleeve Sharpe a standard error of 0.5 [report arithmetic], so admission must rest on the prior sign, the marginal (spanning) statistic and the grade, not on TRAIN Sharpe.

## Existing alphas improve through definitions that add zero fitted parameters

Construction choices move a factor's Sharpe ratio by as much as its sampling error. Across 256 construction variants per factor the investment factor's Sharpe ranges from 0.18 to 0.90 and momentum's from 0.37 to 0.78, the dispersion across design choices exceeds the standard error on average, and the choices that raise gross Sharpe (equal weights, microcaps, all-exchange breakpoints) reverse net of costs ([Soebhag-van Vliet-Verwijmeren 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539824000525)). The standard deviation of true net Sharpe across anomalies is estimated at 0.15 per year against a mean standard error of 0.30 ([Chen-Velikov 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)). The researcher concludes that choosing between two definitions of one signal on a short paired test is dominated by noise unless the paired standard error is below roughly 0.05 [est], which on four years requires a correlation of 0.995 [report arithmetic]. The discipline that follows is one variant per hypothesis, chosen from the literature before any read, with every processing choice counted as a trial. The upgrades below meet that standard; nothing in this section was run on book data.

### Earnings momentum: the slow announcement measure replaces the latest surprise

Drift after the latest earnings surprise is statistically absent outside microcaps. Prices now reflect surprises on the announcement date and drift has been non-existent for large stocks since 2006 ([Martineau 2022](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3111607)); two 2025 top-journal papers that report living drift do not filter microcaps, and a replication finds t 2.18 with all stocks and 1.43 without microcaps, a figure the researcher takes from a magazine article because the working paper itself was not located ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). What survives in the largest 1,000 US stocks is slower: momentum measured as the sum of market-adjusted returns in plus-or-minus two-day windows around all earnings announcements of the prior twelve months earns **4.90% per year (t 2.94) over 2010-2024 at 430% annual one-way turnover**, against 1.15% (t 0.67) at 976% turnover for the latest announcement return; over 1992-2024 it earns 3.51% at 7.91% risk (t 2.51) against 6.52% at 21.79% risk (t 1.70) for standard 12-1 momentum, it does not reverse, and it survives controls for factor momentum ([Gerard-Jehl 2025](https://www.quoniam.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/The-Many-Facets-of-Stock-Momentum.pdf)). This is one recent paper by practitioner authors, but it agrees with the older finding that earnings surprises subsume price momentum among large caps and that earnings momentum made neutral to past returns keeps its spread with lower volatility and no crashes ([Novy-Marx 2015](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/FMFM.pdf)). The recommended change is a new member, `ear_mom_12m`, as the core of the earnings theme, with the filing-clock surprise members collapsed into one momentum-neutral composite carrying a prior near zero in the top 1000. No source tested the measure on the rank 1000-3000 segment, which is where the researcher expects the theme's TRAIN performance to be driven [est].

### Profitability: add R&D back and stop adding quality composites

The 2025 retrospective by the author of the gross-profitability paper finds that operating profit with R&D added back "has the most power predicting returns": in joint regressions it has t 5.69 against 2.57 for cash-based operating profit, it "fully subsumes" the cash-based measure after 2000, and the ordering holds in developed ex-US and emerging markets ([Novy-Marx-Medhat 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33601/w33601.pdf)). The same paper finds that no quality measure tested (return on equity, earnings stability, leverage, payout, quality-minus-junk composites) has significant alpha relative to profitability, and that profitability tilts "explain all the abnormal performance" of alternative value measures, including intangible-adjusted ones. This conflicts with the earlier finding that cash-based profitability subsumes accruals ([Ball et al. 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X16300307)); the researcher notes that both papers share an author pool linked to the same asset manager, so this is a within-school revision and not an independent refutation, and that the Sharpe figures sometimes quoted for the 2016 paper (1.67 against 1.40) come from a secondary summary and are unverified. Profitability is also the one theme that earned more after publication than before, 60 bps per month over the 17 years after 2006 against 31 bps before. The build is blocked on fields: the notes record that fields-v7 has no cost of goods sold or SG&A, so the numerator needs three XBRL tags added, which is a definition change and not new data.

### Value, momentum, low risk and short interest: industry-relative, slower, and honest about what neutralisation removes

For value the best-supported change is measurement within industry. Hedging out 49 industries produces "the largest improvement of 0.42 units in annualized Sharpe ratio" for value-weighted long-short value over 1963-2020, and keeping the sector component produces a better long-short factor in only 20% of bootstrap trials ([Ehsani-Harvey-Li 2023](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P165_Is_sector_neutrality.pdf)); only 32% of the risk of a naive book-to-price portfolio comes from stock selection within industries, and industry neutrality keeps returns while cutting volatility at the price of higher turnover ([Israel-Jiang-Ross 2017](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Working-Papers/AQR--Craftsmanship-Alpha.pdf)). The researcher's book-level estimate is modest, +0.05 to +0.15 Sharpe on the value sleeve and **+0.01 to +0.03 at book level** because the composite is already partly industry-projected [est], and no source compared FF12 with FF49 for a top-3000 daily rank-weighted book. Value portfolios using the most timely price earn alphas of 305 to 378 bps per year over lagged-price value ([Asness-Frazzini 2013](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2054749)), with no post-2015 out-of-sample test found.

For momentum the newest evidence weakens residual momentum as a source of return. Residual momentum is profitable "because it is also a bet against betas", and true firm-specific momentum earned 10.10% per year (t 10.48) over 1965-2000 but **3.45% per year (t 1.53) after 2000** ([Ehsani-Linnainmaa 2022 working paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2023/program/paper/8Ah8THYY)). Because the book projects its composite on beta, the researcher argues it removes the very component that drives residual momentum's alpha, and suggests a prior Sharpe of 0.2-0.4 inside this book [est]; no source measures how much of the improvement survives in a beta- and volatility-neutral portfolio. The intermediate-horizon "echo" is not robust in 37 countries ([Goyal-Wahal 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/is-momentum-an-echo/5E4B893AFD2F110B7347F8F483D28ED3)), so the 21-session skip stays.

For low risk, a value-weighted betting-against-beta portfolio has a five-factor alpha of 24 bps per month (t 1.63), the original strategy commits $1.05 per dollar to stocks in the bottom 1% of market capitalisation, and trading costs remove more than 55% of its profit ([Novy-Marx-Velikov 2022](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/BABAB.pdf)). Betting against correlation shows a five-factor alpha of 0.6% per month (t 5.25) in the US ([Asness-Frazzini-Gormsen-Pedersen 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X1930176X)), but the researcher notes that this evidence uses the same rank weighting that Novy-Marx and Velikov criticise, and no source measured it value-weighted or in the top 1000. The opposing school holds that the low-volatility effect "cannot be explained by other factors such as value, profitability, or exposure to interest rate changes" ([Blitz-van Vliet-Baltussen 2020](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3442749)). For this book the dispute matters little, since the projection on beta and volatility removes the level bet either way; the zero-trial action is an audit of each member's information coefficient after projection. A better beta belongs in the neutraliser: winsorising daily returns at -2 and +4 times the market return with a four-month half-life improves beta prediction by about 5-10% over ordinary least squares ([Welch 2022](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/welch2022simply.pdf)).

For short interest, days-to-cover beats the short-interest ratio and its edge is insensitive to whether turnover is averaged over one, six or twelve months, so a slow denominator lowers turnover for free; value-weighted it earned 0.67% per month (t 2.24) over 1988-2012 and suffered a drawdown during the 2008 short-sale ban ([Hong et al. 2015](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21166/w21166.pdf)). No post-2015 out-of-sample evidence or fee-adjusted result was found.

### Reversal belongs in execution timing, and conditioning mostly explains where alpha is absent

Value-weighted one-month reversal earns 31 bps per month (t 1.68) over 1973-2021, the industry-relative version 74 bps (t 5.40), and the industry-relative version with announcement returns removed **108 bps (t 9.35)**, all gross; the same paper shows that "employing a reversal screen ... can improve expected returns without incurring additional turnover and trading costs" by delaying trades that demand costly liquidity ([Dai-Medhat-Novy-Marx-Rizova 2024](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/RRLP.pdf)). Long-term investors can use short-term signals to time trades they would make anyway "without having to pay additional transaction costs and without capacity limits" ([Israelov-Katz 2011](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/To-Trade-or-Not-to-Trade-Informed-Trading-With-Short-Term-Signals-for-LongTerm-Investors)). One result partly contradicts v6's advice to shrink fast sleeves: a composite of short-term signals applied to about 1,750 large caps with buy and hold rules earns significant net alpha that is robust to implementation lags of several days ([Blitz et al. 2023](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4115411)). The researcher limits the inference: that evidence is for a dedicated fast sleeve with its own trading rules, one of its five signals (analyst revisions) is unavailable, and it does not describe fast signals passed through a 21-day decay into a slow book.

Anomaly returns are about six times higher on earnings announcement days, but the interaction among large stocks is about one quarter of that among small stocks ([Engelberg-McLean-Pontiff 2018](https://rady.ucsd.edu/faculty/directory/engelberg/pub/portfolios/ANOMALIES_NEWS.pdf)). The zero-parameter rule that follows is a "do no harm" one: keep slow-signal positions unchanged through announcements and adopt no risk rule that trims before the event. Conditions that strengthen anomalies (low institutional ownership, high idiosyncratic volatility, high sentiment) mostly identify where arbitrage is costly, so for a large-cap book with costly shorts they explain where alpha cannot be harvested. Missing values should take the cross-sectional median rank, since simple mean imputation performs as well as expectation-maximisation across 159 predictors ([Chen-McCoy 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13071)). Theme timing fails the sizing test: a timing strategy needs a standalone Sharpe above 0.1 to merit any weight beside a strategic portfolio of Sharpe 1.0, and a standalone 0.5 justifies only about a 12% tilt ([Israel-Jiang-Ross 2017](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Working-Papers/AQR--Craftsmanship-Alpha.pdf)); valuation-spread timing inside a multi-style book gave "somewhat disappointing" results ([Asness-Chandra-Ilmanen-Israel 2017](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Contrarian-Factor-Timing-is-Deceptively-Difficult)); and a working paper argues factor momentum's return comes from the unconditional premium, so timing "only adds noise and incurs momentum crashes" ([Leippold-Yang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3517888)). The researcher's power arithmetic puts a timing overlay of true Sharpe 0.3 at t 0.52 on three years and 1.08 on thirteen [est]; on four years it is 0.6 [report arithmetic].

### Priors need class-specific haircuts, not a flat 50%

The US is the only market with a reliable post-publication decline, 62% equal-weighted and 66% value-weighted ([Jacobs-Mueller 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X19301618)). Across 204 anomalies, post-publication gross returns decay about 50%, about 72% when restricted to post-2005, and **93% for cost-mitigated net returns**; the average net return after publication and after 2005 is 4 bps per month, and -2 bps when implementations are restricted to value weighting, before short-sale costs of roughly 10-20 bps per month ([Chen-Velikov 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)). Decay is absent in pre-1926 data unaffected by arbitrage ([Baltussen-van Vliet-van Vliet](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3969743)), which points to crowding and not false discovery as the driver. The researcher's suggested haircuts to published gross performance, all [est], replace the flat 50% of v6 and v7: 65-75% for a generic published anomaly in liquid US names after 2005; 0-30% for the profitability family; 50-60% for slow issuance, investment and value composites; 60-70% on the firm-specific part of momentum; 80-100% for latest-surprise drift in the top 1000; 60-70% for short interest followed by a subtraction of 10-20 bps per month of borrow; and the in-sample to post-sample step of 35-40% plus an allowance for decay still to come for signals with under ten years of out-of-sample data, which includes announcement-based momentum. The researcher adds that the near-zero net figure describes single anomalies traded as stand-alone decile portfolios paying spreads of 67-85 bps, which is a different object from a netted rank-weighted book at 13 bps per dollar; the transferable lesson is that in-sample net performance carries almost no information about which anomaly will be best later, which supports equal theme weights with prior-based selection.

## Combination stays prior-led, and validation moves from single steps to bundles and streams

The combination methods that are defensible at three to eleven years are the ones that estimate only second moments or a single shrinkage intensity. The researchers' break-even arithmetic, using the plug-in approximation that out-of-sample Sharpe is about theta^2 / sqrt(theta^2 + N/T), puts unshrunk fitting of ten theme weights at break-even with equal weights at about 11.6 years (true Sharpe 1.5, equal weights capturing 85%), and fitting of 38 sleeve weights at about 44 years [est]. On four years the blend weight on any fitted theme portfolio is therefore about 0.26 and on a fitted sleeve portfolio about 0.08 [report arithmetic from the researchers' formula], which leaves `ew-theme-v1` as the production default under the owner's TRAIN decision.

The evidence behind the table is mostly negative for complexity. On US and Brazilian equities over 2011-2025 "there is no evidence that hierarchical risk clustering techniques outperform established risk-based approaches", and the hierarchical methods showed higher transaction costs ([Trucios 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00181-026-02900-x)); the often-quoted Monte Carlo advantage of hierarchical risk parity reached the researchers through Wikipedia and not the paper text. In the shrinkage literature an L2 penalty "delivers much higher OOS R2 than a pure L1-penalty Lasso-style approach", a characteristics-sparse kernel does not exist, and individual coefficients have low t-statistics even for the best anomalies ([Kozak-Nagel-Santosh](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24070/w24070.pdf)). Even with ten-year rolling windows and 15 slow characteristics, Fama-MacBeth forecasts have a predictive slope of 0.74, so they are overdispersed by about a quarter ([Lewellen 2015](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2511246)). Machine-learning models trained on one-month returns net to about zero after 2004, while models trained on 3-, 6- or 12-month forward returns trade less and keep significant net returns in 2004-2021 ([Blitz-Hanauer-Hoogteijling-Howard 2023](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4474637)), and 1,056 defensible machine-learning designs span Sharpe ratios from 0.08 to 1.82 ([Chen-Hanauer-Kalsbach](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5031755)). The expected-change column is the researchers' judgement: they found no study applying any of these methods to alpha sleeves with 3-11 years of data and realistic costs.

### Shrinkage says a bad TRAIN result barely moves a prior on four years

In a normal-normal model with prior Sharpe 0.3 and prior standard deviation 0.3, a sleeve with a sample Sharpe of -0.3 keeps 57% of its prior weight on three years and about 1% on eleven [est]; on four years it keeps about 47% [report arithmetic]. The researchers' decision rule follows: on a short TRAIN, never drop or flip a prior-admitted member on the in-sample sign alone; the most the data supports is a weight haircut. Data-driven demotion becomes legitimate only with the backward extension. The hierarchical literature supports the pooling logic: posterior alpha is a shrinkage factor times the estimate, "the more data we have (higher T), the less shrinkage there is", and having data on many factors helps estimate each one ([Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf)). Pure publication bias is small, 12.3% of in-sample returns ([Chen-Zimmermann 2020](https://academic.oup.com/raps/article-abstract/10/2/249/5640503)), and the predictors of decay that can grade a prior in advance are publication year, the number of operations in the signal and sensitivity to outliers ([Falck-Rej-Thesmar 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380)). The researchers could not extract the published prior variances from the Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen text, so the prior standard deviation of 0.3 is a judgement and the key free choice in the recipe.

### Netting and persistence, not mixing, are where combination earns its keep

For a linear long-short book, combining at the signal level and combining sleeve portfolios are identical unless construction is nonlinear, and `ew-theme-v1` already combines at score level. The long-only literature disputes the advantage of integration: robust tests find no statistically significant difference between mixed and integrated approaches ([Leippold-Ruegg 2018](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2887117)), and the often-quoted +1% per year and +40% information ratio for integration come from secondary summaries that the researchers flag for verification ([Fitzgibbons et al. 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2802849)). The practical gain is trade netting, which the platform can report without new parameters as the ratio of combined-score turnover to the weight-averaged turnover of stand-alone sleeves.

Turnover of a score-driven portfolio is governed by the forecast's cross-sectional autocorrelation: turnover scales with sqrt(1 - rho_f), momentum has the lowest forecast autocorrelation and value the highest, and in the source's example smoothing that raises autocorrelation from .90 to .95 cuts turnover to about 71% ([Qian-Sorensen-Hua slides](https://www.northinfo.com/Documents/215.pdf)). The researchers warn that these numbers come from conference slides with partly garbled text extraction and should be checked against the journal article before implementation. Their recipe keeps the level of each sleeve's information coefficient on the prior and takes only the shape of its decay from the data, which uses stocks-times-days observations and is far more precise than sleeve mean returns. The v7 test of persistence-weighted aims lost 0.126 with a standard error of 0.112 on three years; on four years the same test has a standard error near 0.10 [report arithmetic] and on the extended sample near 0.06 [est], so a true effect of 0.10 in either direction becomes detectable only with the backward extension. One consideration favours a retest even on four years. v6 documented that 2020-2022 is a high-volatility window that flatters reversal-type sleeves, and 2023 is a calmer year, so the expanded TRAIN is less biased toward fast liquidity-provision signals than the window that produced the negative verdict; this is an inference from v6's regime table, not a measured result.

Orthogonalising signals against each other is not a free improvement. Symmetric orthogonalisation is order-independent ([Klein-Chow 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062976913000185)), but equal-weighting the orthogonalised signals up-weights low-variance directions, which should carry little Sharpe if near-arbitrage is absent ([Kozak-Nagel-Santosh](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24070/w24070.pdf)); for members correlated at .8-.9 the orthogonalised versions are two to three times amplified differences between near-duplicates [est]. The supported use is to residualise every signal on industry, size and beta, and to keep symmetric orthogonalisation across themes for attribution reports only.

### Validation design: count the looks, separate the streams, and define what a defect costs

With zero-fitted-parameter rules the whole development sample is the test, and the binding problem is the number of looks. Trial counting depends on correlation ("if the 20 strategies tested had a near 1.0 correlation, then the process is equivalent to trying only one strategy"), interactions count in full, and data transformations such as the winsorisation level are "analogous to trying extra variables" ([Arnott-Harvey-Markowitz 2019](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P138_A_backtesting_protocol.pdf)). Hurdles should depend on the prior share of true ideas: with a 10% prior share a 5% error rate needs t = 2.4 on curated strategies, the cutoff is 3.2 to 4.9 when the prior share is zero, and "the 3.0 threshold was never intended as a universal rule" ([Harvey-Liu 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.04269)). The researchers therefore propose two separate alpha-investing streams with separate error budgets, one for literature-signed zero-parameter changes and one for data-mined changes, using the sequential procedures of the online-testing literature ([Foster-Stine alpha-investing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4993364_Alpha-Investing_A_Procedure_for_Sequential_Control_of_Expected_False_Discoveries); [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11418)); the parameter values they give are illustrative, with no finance-specific calibration found.

For fitted combiners, combinatorial purged cross-validation shows lower probability of backtest overfitting than walk-forward, which "exhibits notable shortcomings in false discovery prevention", in a synthetic-data study ([Arian-Norouzi-Seco 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705124011110)). The researchers' layout for the extended sample uses calendar-year groups, two test groups per split, a purge of 21 sessions for monthly labels and an embargo of 5; on TRAIN 2020-2023 alone there are only four calendar-year groups, which gives six splits and three paths [report arithmetic], too few to support a fitted combiner. The researchers also propose a rule for defects, for which they found no literature: an invalid cell is logged and excluded from the trial count; a bug-fix rerun decided without seeing returns replaces the defective cell with no new trial; a rerun where the defect was noticed because the returns looked wrong counts as a new trial, because investigating only disappointing results is a selection mechanism.

### Plan on a live Sharpe of 0.7 to 1.0

The backtest-to-live evidence brackets the haircut. Across 215 bank-marketed systematic strategies the median Sharpe deterioration is 73%, and the most complex strategies lose over 30 percentage points more than the simplest ([Suhonen-Lennkh-Perez 2017](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/43/2/90)); across 72 academic factors post-publication Sharpe is about half the in-sample value and the haircut grows with publication year ([Falck-Rej-Thesmar 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380)); returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower post-publication ([McLean-Pontiff 2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12365)). Applied to the TRAIN Sharpe of 1.405 the ladder runs from 1.24 (publication bias only) through 1.04 and 0.59-0.70 to 0.38 [est]. Because the book's signals are mostly published already, the researchers argue the publication decay is already in the 2020-2022 data and the remaining haircut covers selection across 37 trials, regime luck and cost-model error, which supports the 26-50% range and a planning value of **0.7 to 1.0**, with anything above 1.2 on an eleven-year backtest needing explanation [est]. They found no reliable primary source for the realised net Sharpe of professional multi-signal market-neutral books, so the prior used in their Bayesian version is an assumption.

## The optimiser failed for a diagnosable reason, and capacity comes from slowing the signal

Two v7 recommendations were tried on TRAIN and rejected, and the construction note starts from those results. The cost-shrunk target (v7 R2.2) cut cost per dollar 13-16% and gross Sharpe 10%. The alpha-maximising optimiser (a version of v7 R2.1) cut gross Sharpe 28%, raised cost per dollar 27% and tilted toward illiquid names with high specific volatility; the recipe index records the attempt as 0.54 against 1.33. Neither result contradicts the literature once the mechanism is traced.

The literature attributes optimiser underperformance to three interacting causes. Alpha that the risk model does not span is over-weighted: with a 13-month momentum alpha and a 12-month momentum risk factor the optimiser "will take a disproportionately large bet on stocks that did well 13 months ago, because that bet has alpha but bears no systematic risk" ([Lee-Ruban-Stefek-Yao 2012, MSCI](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/8426a871-688b-4705-ae65-aee7c4c9a0c5)). Constraints interact with the alpha and risk models ([Ceria-Saxena-Stubbs 2012](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/38/2/29)). And forecast error is amplified, with a specific warning about the alpha scaling the optimiser used: "Using a single IC exaggerates volatile securities' alphas" ([Shah 2007, Northfield](https://www.northinfo.com/Documents/247.pdf)). The vendors disagree on the cure: Axioma authors present an alpha alignment factor as a general fix ([Saxena-Stubbs 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259384627_The_Alpha_Alignment_Factor_A_Solution_to_the_Underestimation_of_Risk_for_Optimized_Active_Portfolios)), while MSCI reports that for information ratios "we find both improvements and degradations". The researcher's own derivation identifies the binding gross-leverage cap as the cause of the failed experiment: with alpha equal to IC x sigma x score the unconstrained optimum tilts away from volatile names, but a binding gross cap acts as a soft threshold on alpha per dollar, drops low-volatility names with moderate scores and concentrates in high-volatility, illiquid ones, which reproduces all three symptoms [est; researcher's derivation]. A leverage-constrained investor bidding up risky assets is the same economics as in the betting-against-beta model ([Frazzini-Pedersen](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16601)).

The documented fix is joint regularisation. In a 2000-2023 backtest on 74 S&P 100 stocks a basic Markowitz portfolio earns a Sharpe of 0.19 at 1,145 times annual turnover, while the fully regularised version with a risk target, soft leverage and turnover penalties and robust return and risk terms earns 4.32 at 28 times turnover ([Boyd et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05080)). The authors warn that the data have survivorship bias and the forecasts are synthetic, so only differences between policies carry meaning; the researcher also realigned a turnover column that the text extraction had shifted. The realistic large-scale test points the same way: for a $10bn investor over 1981-2020 the single-period cost-aware optimiser with one tuning layer earns net Sharpe 0.94 at gross leverage 11.2 and "a lower utility than putting all the money in the risk-free asset", a second tuning layer lifts it to 1.11, the method that learns portfolio weights directly earns 1.38, and a decile sort nets -11.87 ([Jensen-Kelly-Malamud-Pedersen](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/SYTF3aaz); published [RFS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhag022)). That study needed two tuning layers and forty years of data; TRAIN here is four years. The researcher therefore recommends a **target-tracking optimiser** as the first experiment: minimise risk-weighted distance to the existing heuristic target plus linear and 1.5-power trading costs and borrow, with no alpha vector and no separate gross cap, so the optimiser only chooses which gaps to close first. The expected effect is cost per dollar down 10-20% for a gross Sharpe loss of 0-3%, or **net Sharpe +0.03 to +0.08 at $1bn and more at $4-8bn** [est]. The researcher is explicit that no published head-to-head test of tracking against alpha maximisation was found, so the recipe rests on inference from the practitioner observation that allowing deviation from the ideal portfolio acts like lowering rebalance frequency ([Israel-Jiang-Ross 2017](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Working-Papers/AQR--Craftsmanship-Alpha.pdf)).

### Cost-shrinking failed because small names carry twice the premium, so judge liquidity rules at $4-8bn

The equal-weighted five-factor long-short portfolio has a Sharpe of 1.08 in small caps against 0.53 in large caps over 1963-2018 ([Blitz-Baltussen-van Vliet 2020](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/130144/Repub_130144_O-A.pdf)), and performance is higher in mid caps than in the top 500 for all strategies in a separate study ([Landier-Simon-Thesmar 2015](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2016/retrieve.php?pdfid=21020&tk=BGQnasd4)). Shrinking targets by cost removes weight exactly where alpha per dollar is highest. The researcher's arithmetic: at $1bn a 15% cost saving is worth about 0.25% per year while 10% of gross return is about 0.75%, so the trade could not pay; at $8bn, where impact is about 2.8 times larger on the fitted capacity curve, the same saving is worth about 0.7% and the experiment is near break-even [est]. Every liquidity-aware rule should therefore use $4bn and $8bn as primary cells. No retrieved study compares equal, rank-linear, score/sigma and score/sigma^2 weights head to head for a long-short stock book; the researcher's derivation shows score/sigma never loses more than about 11% of attainable Sharpe under three alpha-scaling hypotheses while rank weights lose 36% if alpha does not scale with volatility [est; assumes lognormal volatility with coefficient of variation 0.5, not measured on the book], and a zero-trial diagnostic (information coefficient by volatility and liquidity tercile) settles which hypothesis applies.

### Volatility targeting converts improvements into return; it does not raise Sharpe

Across 103 equity strategies the volatility-managed version has the higher Sharpe in 53 cases and the original in 50, and the real-time combination earns a lower certainty equivalent in 72 cases, with momentum, return on equity and betting-against-beta the exceptions ([Cederburg et al. 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)); after costs, managing factors other than the market "generally produces zero abnormal returns and significantly reduces Sharpe ratios" ([Barroso-Detzel 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21000775)). The earlier headline results contain look-ahead bias through an ex-post scaling constant, conventional targeting has turnover "often more than 200% per year", and a conditional rule that scales only in extreme volatility quintiles raises US momentum's Sharpe by 0.17 at about half the turnover, with no significant improvement for other US factors ([Bongaerts-Kang-van Dijk 2020](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/130215/Bongaerts-Kang-van-Dijk-Conditional-volatility-targeting-2020-FAJ.pdf)). One conflicting result exists: a conditional multifactor portfolio managed jointly beats its unconditional counterpart out of sample and net of costs ([DeMiguel-Martin-Utrera-Uppal 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13395)). No study of volatility targeting on a diversified multi-signal market-neutral stock book was found.

The case for an ex-ante risk target is different. Because gross leverage was fixed, the earlier library upgrade cut volatility from 4.71% to 4.13% at unchanged return; under a fixed risk target the same upgrade would have raised net return to about 6.6% at a Sharpe about 0.01 lower [est]. Leverage is not free: net Sharpe declines along the implementable frontier because "more leveraged positions are larger and incur larger trading costs" ([Jensen-Kelly-Malamud-Pedersen](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/SYTF3aaz)). Using the fitted capacity curve, a 6% volatility target at $1bn costs about 0.04 of Sharpe and lifts net return from 5.8% to 8.2% [est; ignores financing spread on added gross and caps binding more often]. The tail evidence argues against mechanical deleveraging: in August 2007 a manager who cut leverage after the loss "would not have been able to recoup all of its losses" despite the reversal ([Khandani-Lo](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). The industry leverage figures in the notes (645% gross for quantitative funds) come from secondary sources that do not name their originals and should be re-sourced before use.

### The book trades about nine times faster than published cost-aware portfolios

The book trades 3.8% of gross per day, about 80% per month; the $10bn learned portfolio trades 0.32 of NAV per month at gross leverage 3.60, about 8.9% of gross per month, roughly one ninth of the book's rate [est]. The researcher cautions that the comparison shows direction and rough size, not a target, since those studies rebalance monthly with quadratic costs. The theory ranks the levers: capacity has elasticity 1 with respect to price impact and **elasticity 2 with respect to the speed at which the signal fades** ([Landier-Simon-Thesmar 2015](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2016/retrieve.php?pdfid=21020&tk=BGQnasd4)), so slowing the signal is a stronger capacity lever than slowing the trade. Moving from a one-day to a five-day trading horizon raises estimated momentum capacity from $65bn to $320bn ([Ratcliffe-Miranda-Ang 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2861324)).

Four turnover recipes follow, each with pre-registered arms. Rank-space hysteresis has the best evidence: moving from buy-20/hold-20 to buy-10/hold-50 gave "a slight decrease in the gross alpha" and lifted net alpha above 6% per year for a short-term composite ([Hanauer et al. 2022, Robeco](https://cdn.uc.assets.prezly.com/dbe30eb4-6b9f-4e86-9633-a127867241f6/-/inline/no/20220530%20Beyond%20Fama-French%20-%20alpha%20from%20short-term%20signals%20May%202022%20-%20GB.pdf)); for the slower book the researcher expects turnover down 20-40% for gross Sharpe down 2-5% [est]. A one-sided band trades to the near edge of a no-trade zone whose width scales as cost^(1/2) with a stable forecast and cost^(1/3) in stochastic settings ([Isichenko 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15239)), with expected cost down 5-15% [est]. A horizon-weighted aim replaces each sleeve's score by its expected cumulative alpha over the realised holding period of about 50 days, as prescribed for low-turnover portfolios ([Shah 2007](https://www.northinfo.com/Documents/247.pdf)), with turnover down 10-25% [est]. A best-trade-first rule under a turnover budget has no published evidence. At the asset levels that matter, re-tuning the trade rate per asset level is expected to add nothing at $1bn and **+0.03 to +0.10 net Sharpe at $8bn** [est].

The book's own capacity curve (net Sharpe 1.45, 1.41, 1.36, 1.24, 1.08 at $0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8bn) is fitted within 0.03 by 1.58 - 0.175 x sqrt(NAV in $bn), dollar profit is still rising at $8bn, and net Sharpe 1.0 is reached at about $11bn by extrapolation beyond the tested range [est]. Compared with the hedge-fund evidence that doubling fund size lowers alpha by 17 bps per year per standard deviation of skill after 2008 ([Bollen-Joenvaara-Kauppila 2024](https://cfr.ivo-welch.org/forthcoming/papers/bollen2024decreasing.pdf)), the book's modelled decay of 20-66 bps per doubling is at or above average, so the curve is not obviously optimistic [est]. Capacity claims should hold under a stress cost model until live fills exist, because a trader who misjudges mid-cap liquidity suffers "a huge impact on performance" ([Landier-Simon-Thesmar 2015](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2016/retrieve.php?pdfid=21020&tk=BGQnasd4)).

### Execution and the short side: the close is cheap only for small participants, and fees are concentrated

The closing auction grew from 3.11% of daily dollar volume in 2010 to 7.48% in 2018, its price deviates from the 4pm midquote by 8.12 bps on average (2.66 bps for large stocks, 20.6 bps for small), and deviations "reverse almost fully overnight" ([Bogousslavsky-Muravyev](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)); by 2024 auctions matched about $50bn per day, roughly 9% of volume ([BMLL 2025](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)), and unfilled interest at the close is about 3.3% of executed volume for Russell 1000 names and over 6.3% outside ([NYSE Research 2026](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/behind-the-record-volumes-a-hidden-opportunity)). Those averages describe a marginal participant: an order of 1% of daily volume sent entirely to the close is about 11% of auction volume [est]. Measured institutional impact is about 17 bps at 2% of daily volume and 28 bps at 6% ([Israel-Jiang-Ross 2017](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Working-Papers/AQR--Craftsmanship-Alpha.pdf)), so spreading over days dominates concentrating in one print. With daily bars only, the backtest cannot see intraday impact, and the researcher's condition for any execution change is that its ranking agrees under two cost models. One extra day of delay costs about 13% of alpha for a five-day half-life and about 1% for a 63-day half-life [est], so delay matters only for the fast, capacity-bound sleeves.

On the short side, each of the eight cheapest fee deciles averages below 32 bps per year while the tenth averages 568 bps, and eight anomalies "effectively disappear" within the 80% of stocks with low fees, except gross profitability ([Drechsler-Drechsler, NBER w20282](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20282/w20282.pdf)). Across 162 anomalies the average long-short return is 0.14% per month before short-sale costs, "due to the short leg", and -0.01% after fees, and anomalies "are not profitable even before fees if the high-fee observations, representing 12% of stock dates, are excluded" ([Muravyev-Pearson-Pollet 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13501)); the researcher read only the abstract of that paper. The opposing finding is that combined long legs reach a Sharpe of 1.1 against under 0.7 for short legs and no short leg adds significant alpha over the long legs ([Blitz-Baltussen-van Vliet 2020](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/130144/Repub_130144_O-A.pdf)). The researcher reconciles them: gross short-leg alpha is concentrated in the names that cost most to short [est]. Without fee data the documented proxy is short interest divided by institutional ownership, "a rough measure of the demand for shorting ... relative to available lending supply", whose cheap-minus-expensive portfolio earns 1.48% per month, close to the fee-sorted result, though it is "a noisy signal". If shorts were spread evenly over the published fee distribution the drag would be about 0.12 Sharpe units, and if the backtest charges a flat general-collateral rate, **modelled net Sharpe is overstated by up to about 0.1** [est]. A fee-schedule stress by proxy decile is therefore a realism item that precedes any short-side optimisation, and rules that down-weight expensive shorts must be judged net of that schedule, because on gross returns they will look harmful.

## A faster platform removes repeated work, and mined alphas face hurdles of t 3.5 to 4.6

Every fast public research system uses the same five ideas: a declarative expression language compiled to a graph, de-duplication of shared sub-expressions across the batch, a cache keyed at the sub-expression level, fixed-width columnar storage that can be sliced by date, and one definition that runs in batch and streaming modes. The largest measured lever in a public system is caching: building 14 factors on 800 stocks over 2007-2020 takes 147.0 seconds on one CPU without cache, 47.6 with an expression cache and **7.4 with expression and dataset caches**, about 20 times faster ([Yang et al. 2020, Qlib](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11189)). That is the authors' own benchmark. The other headline numbers are weaker evidence still: a compiler that fuses operators and eliminates common sub-expressions quotes about 170 times pandas on the 101 alphas in float32 ([KunQuant](https://github.com/Menooker/KunQuant)), and a vendor reports a median 15.5 times speed-up with 32 of 101 alphas excluded and no correctness comparison ([DolphinDB tutorial](https://docs.dolphindb.com/en/Tutorials/wq101alpha.html)); both are self-reported and were read through machine summaries. The researcher's judgement is that these speed-ups will not transfer one for one, because they are measured against slow Python baselines, mostly in single precision, and free to reorder floating-point reductions; the platform requires double precision and byte reproducibility, so the gain to expect comes from removing repeated work and not from vector instructions. No public measurement of expression caching under a bit-reproducibility constraint was found.

The design that follows has four parts. The cache key moves from the alpha to the node, as a hash of operator, parameters and child keys, after canonicalising expressions (operand order, constant folding, collapsing nested ranks) so that mined candidates differing only in form neither defeat the cache nor inflate the trial count; an evolutionary miner that fingerprints pruned alphas and reuses stored fitness is a published precedent ([Cui et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16196)). Evaluation runs in date blocks with a lookback halo, which is the design of Zipline's chunked pipeline engine ([Zipline engine](https://zipline.ml4trading.io/_modules/zipline/pipeline/engine.html)) and is what lets a longer panel fit in local memory. A two-tier backtest screens every candidate with a vectorised weights-times-returns pass and a linear cost term, and runs the exact replay only for finalists. And construction grids run in lockstep over shared signal and risk inputs. The published yardstick says the exact replay is not the bottleneck: a single-period convex problem with 1,500 assets and 50 factors solves in under half a second and a 32-core machine runs five years times 64 parameter choices in under ten minutes, by the authors' estimates and not a measured benchmark ([Boyd et al. 2017](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/cvx_portfolio.pdf)); warm starts cut quadratic-programme time 5.8 to 7 times on the portfolio problem ([Stellato et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08013)). A cheap screen carries a documented risk of steering search to a "proxy optimum" ([Yu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01789)), so the screen must be validated once by rank-correlating its net Sharpe with the exact replay across the existing library. The researcher's loop-time budget of well under one minute per idea is a target built from v7's measured timers and the published caching results; it has not been measured on the platform.

### Score candidates on marginal contribution, which needs no full backtest

"It is optimal to include an asset i in a portfolio if and only if S_i >= rho_{i,p} x S_p" ([Benhamou-Guez 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09864)). From this the researcher derives the candidate's appraisal ratio against the book, (S_c - rho x S_b) / sqrt(1 - rho^2), and the best attainable combined Sharpe, sqrt(S_b^2 + AR^2): with a book Sharpe of 1.2, a candidate with Sharpe 0.6 adds 12% at zero correlation, 2% at correlation 0.3 and nothing at 0.5 [est]. Correlation matters more than standalone Sharpe, which is the same conclusion the new-alpha section reached from a different direction. A parameter-free robustness gate is also available: the researcher recovered from a 2026 preprint's tables that the WorldQuant sub-universe test equals 0.75 x sqrt(N_sub / N_full) times the full-universe Sharpe, reproducing the printed limits to two decimals ([AgonAlpha](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11250)); this is a rule inferred from data, since the official documentation is behind a login. Redundancy cut-offs in public use range from 0.70 on profit-and-loss correlation to 0.99 on information-coefficient correlation ([RD-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15155)). No published study measures how well residual information coefficient or spanning alpha predicts the realised change in net book Sharpe after costs, so the scoring rule needs in-house calibration on the existing library.

### Automated alpha mining: generation is cheap, judgement is not, and US evidence is thin

Reported mining results come overwhelmingly from China A-share indices, price-volume inputs, short test windows and authors' own baselines. A reinforcement-learning miner reports a test information coefficient of 0.0725 on the CSI300 ([Yu et al. 2023, AlphaGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12964)); a later paper re-running that style of baseline under a different label and a walk-forward window scores it at 0.0209 ([Shi et al. 2025, AlphaForge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18394)). The researcher treats such cross-paper re-evaluation as the closest thing to independent replication, and it is unflattering. On US large caps the verified figures are small: an information coefficient of 0.0056 on the S&P 500 for one LLM-driven system, read through a machine summary ([Tang et al. 2025, AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789)), and 0.0162-0.0172 on the NASDAQ 100 over an 18-month test for another ([Li et al. 2025, RD-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15155)). The platform-scale result, 17 of 60 submissions graded at the top tier on the US TOP3000 with Sharpe up to 3.48, is entirely in-sample, and its own notes flag one year contributing about 41% of in-sample profit for one alpha ([AgonAlpha](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11250)). None of these studies reports a deflated Sharpe, a probability of overfitting or a trial count, and the researcher found **no independent peer-reviewed replication of any mining method on US equities with realistic costs**, which is the largest gap in this literature.

The independent evaluations are consistent. Language models generate syntactically valid factors reliably, but as zero-shot judges of factor quality their accuracy is "generally close to random guessing" and evolutionary capacity saturates at about 20 candidates per round ([Luo et al. 2026, AlphaBench](https://www.cs.cityu.edu.hk/~cliu644/HomePage/doc/AlphaBench/AlphaBench_PDF.pdf)). The one backtest-versus-live comparison shows Sharpe roughly halving, from about 1.53 to about 0.70, but the live window is 3.5 months and the headline numbers come from a snippet because the table did not extract cleanly ([AlphaCrafter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05580)). A survey warns that "increasing autonomy mainly increases the speed at which systems fit historical noise" unless fitness controls for repeated testing, and that model rationales create "narrative overfitting" ([Yu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01789)). Look-ahead contamination is measurable: a one-standard-deviation rise in a look-ahead proxy raises the marginal effect of a model's forecast by about 37% of baseline, a figure taken from a search snippet ([Gao-Jiang-Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)). A full LLM-driven search costs under $10 in one system ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15155)) and $7.5 to $74.4 in another ([Shi-Duan-Li 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11122)). Generation is therefore the cheap part. The defensible design for a small US team is a constrained grammar of typed templates with a few free slots, a hard complexity cap, fitness that is marginal to the book and net of the linear cost model, successive halving across fidelities, and a language model used as a proposer of hypotheses only, shown neither dates, tickers, returns nor hidden-block results. The platform's language has about 90 operators against 21 in the published miner, so its false-discovery exposure is much larger, and mined price-volume alphas are short-horizon and high-turnover by construction, which runs against the v8 objective of lower turnover and higher capacity.

### Multiple-testing control must scale with the size of the search

The literature disagrees on how many published findings are false. One side derives thresholds of t 3.8 and 3.4 from more than two million strategies and estimates that about 45% of unadjusted rejections are false, a figure read from a snippet ([Chordia-Goyal-Saretto 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2134/5739455)); the other bounds the false discovery rate among |t| > 2 findings at 25%, with a tighter bound of 9% ([Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15365)), and finds that raised hurdles are weakly identified while empirical-Bayes shrinkage is well identified ([Chen, Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.03083)). Both sides are peer-reviewed and the dispute is unresolved. They agree on what matters here: signals selected at |t| > 2 lose about half their return out of sample whether they came from peer review or from mining, and value-weighted decay is about 60% ([Chen-Lopez-Lira-Zimmermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317)).

The researcher's hurdle table, computed from the deflated-Sharpe formula and Bonferroni at 5% with independent trials assumed, gives the price of search. The four-year column is this report's extension.

| Effective trials | Expected max z under the null | Null best annual Sharpe, 3y [est] | 4y [report arithmetic] | 10y [est] | Bonferroni t [est] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1.58 | 0.91 | 0.79 | 0.50 | 2.81 |
| 100 | 2.53 | 1.46 | 1.27 | 0.80 | 3.48 |
| 1,000 | 3.26 | 1.88 | 1.63 | 1.03 | 4.06 |

On four years a mined alpha that is the best of 1,000 effective trials needs a standalone annual Sharpe above about 1.6 merely to beat the expected maximum of pure noise. The researcher calls this the strongest argument for granting the longer panel to mining before anything else, since a ten-year sample nearly halves every hurdle, and it means the owner's backward-extension decision gates the miner more than any algorithm choice does. The proposed admission rule has three classes keyed to a registry field for origin: a literature prior with a canonical definition passes a sign veto and t >= 2 on the spanning statistic and costs one trial; a human idea with a bounded grid counts every cell; a machine-mined candidate must clear the Bonferroni value for the campaign's effective trial count, **3.5 to 4.6 for 100 to 10,000 trials**, applied to the marginal statistic, with the campaign budget fixed in advance. The researcher's pipeline self-test, permuting returns across dates and planting one synthetic signal of known strength, is a proposal and not a cited method, and no published hurdle calibrated for machine-mined daily US alphas was found. A holdout-free bound based on Rademacher complexity appears in a 2025 textbook, but the researcher reached it only through a secondary blog and did not verify the formula ([Balaena Quant note](https://medium.com/balaena-quant-insights/rademacher-anti-serum-ras-08cef83f7102)).

The order of work follows from the evidence that about 20 backtest iterations suffice to find a false strategy at the 5% level and that "feature importance is a research tool, and backtesting is not" ([Lopez de Prado 2018](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3104816)): first one specification in and one report out; then the node cache; then the validated screen; then the marginal test; then trial accounting by origin class; then date-blocked evaluation for the long panel; and only then a constrained miner. The hypothesis, the fields and templates opened to search, the campaign budget, admission of any mined alpha and every hidden-block query stay manual.

## Conclusion

The three objectives of the sprint, higher net Sharpe, higher capacity and lower turnover, point to the same place, and that is the main thing the v8 notes add to v6 and v7. Signal persistence is the only lever with a quadratic capacity payoff, persistent signals are the ones that survive costs and publication, and the definitional upgrades with the best recent evidence (announcement-window momentum, within-industry value, slow-denominator days-to-cover) all lower turnover as a side effect. The mirror image is equally clear: the fashionable additions, mined price-volume formulas, fitted weights, timing overlays and volatility scaling, raise turnover or parameters and have weak or negative net evidence on US large caps. The sprint's scarce resource is therefore trials, not ideas. The build list below costs about 66 pre-registered trials on top of the 37 already spent; at roughly 100 trials the expected best Sharpe of pure noise on four years is about 1.27 if trials were independent [report arithmetic], and although correlated construction cells make the effective count far lower, the arithmetic says trials should be budgeted like capital and spent on bundles.

The owner's two decisions interact. Moving 2023 into TRAIN is a modest gain in precision that arrives with a cost: 2023 was seen twice, and 2024 stays hidden with the same history, so the only evidence that can still surprise anyone is 2025 onward and the unread years before 2020. The backward extension changes the regime of inference and not merely its precision: on about ten years, data-driven demotion of weak members, theme-level fitted weights, a fair retest of persistence-weighted aims and any mining campaign all become legitimate, while on four years none of them is. The extension also carries the one risk the literature cannot price for this platform, the quality of point-in-time and delisting data before 2020. A sensible sequence is to spend the weeks of that decision on the zero-trial work (validation kit, realism diagnostics, platform speed), which is needed under either outcome, and to hold the single read of the pre-2020 block until the book and its accepted improvements are frozen.
