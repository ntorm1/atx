# SEC bulk archive feasibility for historical listings (2026-09-23)

Scope: official SEC documentation and an SEC-maintained example only. No archive download, database change, vendor purchase, contact, or email was made. This checks whether the Financial Statement and Notes (FSN) bulk archives can establish a survivorship-safe US listed-common-equity universe, especially from 2012 onward. The existing [SEC bulk research](historical-listing-sec-bulk-research.md) contains the wider source audit.

## Decision

**Yes, FSN contains dated filing observations of symbol, registered security title, and exchange. No, FSN alone cannot establish complete historical listing membership, listing/delisting intervals, or vendor instrument identity.** It is a feasible bulk *evidence enrichment* source; it is not a historical exchange security master. A 2024 observed filing cannot make its tuple valid in 2012, and an absent tuple cannot establish that a security was unlisted.

## What is included

- The [FSN data dictionary](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfsn_1.pdf), sections 2, 3 and 5, explicitly includes `TXT`, one row per **nonnumeric** XBRL fact, as well as `SUB`, `TAG`, and `DIM`. This is the larger Financial Statement **and Notes** product; the compact numeric Financial Statement Data Sets are a different product. `TAG` defines possible tags; finding a name there is not evidence of a reported value.
- The [SEC-maintained narrative-facts notebook](https://github.com/sec-gov/python-for-dera-financial-datasets/blob/main/examples/5_Find_Narrative_Text_Facts.ipynb) includes saved `TXT` output for accession `0002004032-24-000003`. In source context `c-1`, the actual rows are `Security12bTitle = Common Stock, par value $0.01 per share`, `SecurityExchangeName = NYSE`, and `TradingSymbol = MOS`, each with `version=dei/2023`, `ddate=20240229`, and `dimh=0x00000000`. This demonstrates inclusion of the target text facts in a published example. It does **not** measure prevalence in any quarter or prove that every ZIP contains all three facts.
- [FSN download page](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/financial-statement-notes-data-sets) lists archives from January 2009 through August 2026, including every 2012 quarter. The dictionary says scope begins April 15, 2009 and that 2009 Q1 has headers only. Archives switched from quarterly to monthly updates in November 2020, with older monthly files consolidated after a year. Availability of 2012 archives does not mean these specific tags were populated then.

## Why this does not close the historical universe gap

1. **Tagging coverage changed.** The [SEC's cover-page tagging compliance guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/fast-act-modernization-simplification-regulation-s-k) gives phase-in dates for fiscal periods ending June 15, 2019, 2020, and 2021 by filer category. The [adopting release](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2019/33-10618.pdf) says exchange information was among cover-page data previously not required to be XBRL tagged. This makes pre-rollout exchange/class tuple coverage especially uncertain. The 2012 archive exists, but no inspected 2012 `TXT` rows prove target-fact coverage or completeness.
2. **The unit is a filing fact, not a listing event.** `TXT.ddate` is a rounded fact date and `SUB.period` is a reporting period; neither is an exchange listing start/end or a market observation date. `SUB.filed` and `SUB.accepted` describe filing timing. The [SEC's EDGAR access guide](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) says some submissions begun after 5:30 p.m. ET are disseminated next business day. The FSN posting itself arrives later. Keep reported period, filing acceptance, dissemination/usable-at policy, and bulk retrieval timestamp distinct.
3. **Class and exchange identity need context.** The [August 2026 EDGAR XBRL Guide](https://www.sec.gov/files/edgar/filer-information/specifications/xbrl-guide-2026-08-14.pdf), section 3.2.4, says securities are not limited to common shares, classes may use `StatementClassOfStockAxis` or `ClassesOfShareCapitalAxis`, multiple exchanges use `EntityListingsExchangeAxis`, and title/exchange facts pair within the same context. It also says no instance type requires `Security12bTitle` or `Security12gTitle`. `SecurityExchangeName` uses EDGAR exchange acronyms for national exchanges, not a universal MIC or all trading venues. A CIK identifies a filer, not a permanent share-class/instrument ID. Joining three facts merely by accession, or joining a vendor instrument merely by ticker, can invent a security tuple.
4. **The archive is reconstructed.** The [FSN page](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/financial-statement-notes-data-sets) reports refreshed 2009–2018 data and 2024 corrections to 2010–2013 files. Current archive bytes therefore are not a point-in-time copy of the earlier bulk publication. This does not invalidate as-filed evidence, but prevents treating today's ZIP as an exact historical availability record.

## Feasible bounded next step

If filing-observation enrichment is useful, stream only `TXT` rows for `TradingSymbol`, `SecurityExchangeName`, `Security12bTitle` (plus `Security12gTitle`/`NoTradingSymbolFlag` where relevant), then join referenced `SUB` accessions and `DIM` hashes. Preserve accession, CIK/co-registrant, form, context, dimension members, all raw values, filing dates, and the source archive. Reject conflicting or incomplete same-context tuples rather than combining values across contexts. Sample one 2012 quarter, one pre-rollout quarter, one rollout quarter, and one post-2021 quarter before scaling; measure actual target-tag presence and class ambiguity, not `TAG` definitions. The archive is large (e.g. the published 2012 Q1 ZIP is 424.02 MB), so inspect ZIP metadata and budget streamed extraction first. This yields auditable observations only. A separate dated exchange listing/security-master or event source is still required for certified 2012-onward membership and delisting intervals.
