# Derived revision preservation: static correctness audit

Date: 2026-09-20. Scope: active standardization -> quarterly derived metrics -> daily market panel -> exports/projections. This is a source audit and hand calculation, not a warehouse measurement. No application code, migrations, registry, tests, DB connections, production processes, network calls, or source data were changed. Codebase graph tools were unavailable; source discovery used `rg` and file reads. Read `program.md` and `continuation-queue.md`; the controller's continuation instructions govern current ownership and migration reservations. **Latest controller update: core_metric_breadth needs no migration, so 0315 is now available for the PIT state schema, subject to controller ownership.**

## Finding: Critical historical correctness failure

The active standardized table retains numeric revisions, but the derived engine reduces them to the globally latest revision before computing. It then replaces every previously materialized derived row in its selected scope. Historical derived values cannot be reconstructed by applying an ASOF cutoff to that result. The cutoff avoids using the later amended value too early; it instead removes a previously knowable value, causing missing metrics or fallback to an older fiscal period. Dependency windows propagate the loss to additional periods and downstream ratios, growth and per-share metrics.

The daily panel independently repeats the latest-only mistake for direct standardized inputs and DEI share history. Fixing only the derived writer cannot make the complete consumer path PIT correct.

## Hand-worked amendment counterexample

Assume one security, a single standardized revenue rule/source, quarterly basis, ordinary calendar quarter ends, no collisions, and the following availability dates at 12:00. This isolates revision semantics from alias, fiscal calendar, identity and source-vintage questions. Values use arbitrary consistent monetary units.

| Quarter end | Original revenue | Original available_at |
|---|---:|---|
| 2023-06-30 | 70 | 2023-08-10 |
| 2023-09-30 | 80 | 2023-11-10 |
| 2023-12-31 | 90 | 2024-02-10 |
| 2024-03-31 | 100 | 2024-05-10 |
| 2024-06-30 | 110 | 2024-08-10 |
| 2024-09-30 | 120 | 2024-11-10 |
| 2024-12-31 | 130 | 2025-02-10 |

On **2025-03-10**, an amendment changes **2024-06-30 revenue from 110 to 160**. Standardization produces revision 1 (110, available 2024-08-10, latest=false, valid_to=2025-03-10) and revision 2 (160, available 2025-03-10, latest=true). Other rows retain their original clocks.

The catalog's `revenue_ttm = ttm(revenue)` is at `seeds/derived_metric_definitions.csv:12`. `derived_dsl.py:485-492` requires four populated dense-quarter rows, sums their values, and takes the maximum of their availability clocks.

| Target quarter | Correct original state | Correct new state at amendment | Current writer after refresh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-03-31 | 340 at 2024-05-10 | Unchanged | 340 at 2024-05-10 |
| 2024-06-30 | 380 at 2024-08-10 | 430 at 2025-03-10 | Only 430 at 2025-03-10 |
| 2024-09-30 | 420 at 2024-11-10 | 470 at 2025-03-10 | Only 470 at 2025-03-10 |
| 2024-12-31 | 460 at 2025-02-10 | 510 at 2025-03-10 | Only 510 at 2025-03-10 |

At **2025-02-20 22:00**, the right daily revenue TTM is 460. After today's derived rebuild, `latest_by_code` has no eligible TTM event for the last three target periods: all have been moved to March 10. Its ASOF result falls back to **340 from 2024-03-31**. With market cap fixed at 4,600 and all share/bar inputs unaffected, `ps_ttm = safe_div(market_cap, revenue_ttm)` (`derived_metric_definitions.csv:149`) changes from **10** to **13.5294117647** for that already-past date. If the available source history began in 2023 Q3, the surviving 340 row would not exist and PS would become NULL instead. Neither result is the information known on February 20.

At March 10, correctly rebuilt daily state should switch to **510 for 2024 Q4** (PS 9.0196078431). Updating only the derived output for the *amended* 2024 Q2 period is insufficient: newest-period precedence would retain Q4=460, which is also wrong. Every target window containing the changed input needs its own amendment event. At a February 20 quarterly export cutoff, standardized Q2 revenue correctly remains 110, while derived TTM for Q2/Q3/Q4 is absent after the current rebuild.

The direct standardized daily branch has a separate simple failure: between 2024-08-10 and 2024-11-10, latest-only selection removes Q2=110 and daily consumers can fall back to Q1=100. A later Q3 filing masks this direct-input defect after November; it does not repair the TTM defect.

After the March 10 amendment, a daily raw-revenue selector must still use Q4=130, the newest visible fiscal period, rather than the newly arrived Q2=160. An availability-only latest-event selector would get this wrong. At that same event the daily **derived** selector must use Q4 TTM=510, because the old Q2 revision affects the newer Q4 window. These are complementary requirements.

### Dependency clocks and fallback transitions

`gross_profit_q` uses `coalesce(gross_profit__1004, revenue - cost_of_revenue_cogs)` (catalog line 2); `gross_profit_ttm` uses `ttm(gross_profit_q)` (line 14); `gross_margin` divides gross profit TTM by revenue TTM (line 55). Assume no direct gross profit and cost=60 for each quarter, known on the corresponding original revenue dates. For 2024 Q4, correct gross profit TTM is **220**, later **270**. Gross margin therefore has two states: **220/460** at February 10 and **270/510** at March 10. The current writer retains only the latter. Dependency clocks correctly carry March 10 through the latest computation; they do not preserve the earlier computation.

Growth expands impact beyond one metric's local frame: `revenue_growth_yoy = yoy(revenue_ttm)` (catalog line 73) uses the current and four-quarter-lagged TTM. A changed TTM at bucket p affects growth at p and p+4, as those target periods become observable. Transitive event propagation is required; global `max_lag` from one expression alone does not describe the raw-fact impact of the entire DAG.

The DSL's `coalesce` clock currently uses the selected branch only (`derived_dsl.py:446-455`). A previously valid preferred branch invalidated at t2 can switch to a fallback whose numeric inputs were known at t1. The resulting state becomes selectable at **t2**, even if its arithmetic inputs have a maximum clock of t1. A revision design must retain the selection/invalidation event clock; otherwise fallback can be backdated. Invalid/null states are needed for control flow as well as numerical output.

## Existing history is not an alternate solution

There is a genuine revision source in `fundamental_standardized`; there is no alternate derived-state reader on this traced path. The standardized build ledger carries counts, hashes and status, not historical derived snapshots. The only derived writes found in application modules are the engine insert/delete above. Downstream readers use that table directly.

`publication.py:101-105` publishes `derived_metric_values` keyed by `derived_value_id`; `release_query` at lines 156-166 selects the live table as-is. `publish_release` at lines 318-369 creates immutable release directories sharing a DB snapshot. Such a release preserves what happened to be materialized when it was cut, but **does not generate every filing event**, and no traced consumer unions old release files to rebuild PIT states. No existing release inventory was examined or assumed. A previously cut release cannot establish complete filing-time history.

One important upstream limit: standardization is revision-complete for the admitted numeric candidates, not a universal withdrawal stream. `_standardization_set_based.py:157`, analogous statement/raw branches, and combination completeness at line 758 filter missing values/states. No downstream design can infer an explicit fact withdrawal solely because a later filing omits the concept. An explicit known invalidation requires a clocked status/tombstone contract upstream. This does not weaken the numeric-amendment counterexample, which uses fully supported rows.

Clock scope is equally important: the example assumes the upstream `available_at` is the intended knowledge event. A filing acceptance/publication timestamp, a vendor delivery timestamp, and the local observation/backfill `source_loaded_at` are different clocks. Reconstructing states from modeled upstream availability preserves that modeled chronology; it does not prove a vendor/security mapping/price value was historically available then. Preserve clock provenance and source-observation stamps separately, never use local rebuild time to redate filing revisions, and do not certify historical vintages from the bar `trade_date + 22h` convention or current bulk delivery alone. If an output promises local-observation-time replay, its cutoff must additionally respect that explicit clock and needs the corresponding retained versions.

## Bounded implementable repair

This needs coordinated writer/schema/consumer work, not removal of a WHERE clause. Implement it as a dedicated task before certifying the reconstructed market/factor panels.

1. **Define canonical input state intervals.** Within a bounded security batch, deterministically rank standardized candidates for each code/basis/period at each availability timestamp, using explicit source/rule precedence and stable IDs as final tie-breaks. Use all revisions; never prefilter on latest or nonnull value when a clocked invalid state exists. Derive half-open intervals `[available_at, next_available_at)`. Dense quarter bucketing remains, but bucket representative-period metadata must itself be event-aware: a later observed period end must not rename a historical output before it was known. Preserve the existing missing-quarter rules. Do not use future rows to decide past collisions or output-period existence.

2. **Generate only affected target/event keys.** For each metric in topological order, map direct input revision events to the target buckets that may consume them. A conservative bounded offset set 0..local `max_lag` is safe; exact DSL dependency offsets reduce work. TTM uses 0..3; a plain four-quarter lag uses offset 4. Join to this security's observed target buckets/metadata events and union/deduplicate `(security, metric, target_bucket, event_at)`. Include target-appearance/representative-period-change events so old inputs can first yield an output when its target becomes observable. Dependency metric states, including invalidations and lineage changes, feed the same process for downstream metrics. Do not form security x global events or even one security's events x whole history x all metrics.

3. **Evaluate bounded event-local frames.** Chunk the target/event keys. For each, form only `[target_bucket - local_max_lag, target_bucket]` dense frame rows and interval-join input states valid at that event. Evaluate the DSL using partitions that include target/event identity; emit only the target row. Dependency states must be selected as of the same event **per dependency period**, never independent `max(value)`/`max(clock)` pivots across revisions. Read enough predecessor interval state when chunking by time; a chunk boundary cannot erase the seed state. SQL carries frames and values; Python carries bounded keys, counts and registry metadata only.

4. **Persist state transitions, including invalid output.** Keep every relevant availability event where the selected input/lineage/status changes, even if the numeric result is identical. Store `available_at = event_at` for the state transition and, optionally, a separate max arithmetic-input clock. Propagate control-flow/absence clocks. When `safe_div` receives a denominator amended to zero, retain a NULL/invalid state at the amendment, rather than dropping it and allowing the old quotient to persist. A window missing a required quarter is invalid, and a later arrival can make it valid. Distinguish an explicitly invalid state from an input never observed; do not invent filing withdrawals from omission.

5. **Govern the schema and identity.** `migrations/bodies_0302.py:117-131` currently makes `value DOUBLE NOT NULL` and has no validity/status/revision group. Prefer making the canonical derived state table able to represent nullable value plus explicit `value_status`/reason and revision metadata (`valid_to`, sequence/count/latest as conveniences). Alternatively add a canonical state table and retain valid-only `derived_metric_values` as a compatibility surface, but **all PIT consumers must then read the state table**; valid-only compatibility rows must not be used for ASOF selection. Coordinate migration 0315 with root under its latest reservation update; do not modify 0302 retroactively.

   Use a typed canonical serialization for the revision group `(source, security_id, metric_code, metric_window, target_period_identity, definition_fingerprint)` and a state identity adding the availability event. Resolve same-timestamp candidates to one deterministic state before hashing. Keep selected input stable IDs, clocks, values/statuses, dependency state IDs and definition semantics in a separately deterministic `inputs_hash`; avoid delimiter ambiguity and mutable revision counters as the sole identity. Do not use run ID, load time or generated revision sequence in the stable key. If source corrections reuse an availability timestamp, replace that deterministic event state during a governed rebuild; retaining separate warehouse-load vintages would require an explicit additional system-time dimension and is not implied by a filing-time event key.

6. **Repair consumers together.** Daily standardized/DEI branches must use historical states and deterministic newest-visible-period precedence. Select a **whole struct/state ID** rather than `arg_max(nullable_value, ...)`, so an invalid newest-period state supersedes the previously valid one instead of being skipped. The same applies to quarterly export (`panel_export.py:223`) and dependency pivots. Ranking precedes any valid-value filter. Retain newest-period precedence for older-period amendments, while derived events update every affected newer target window. Factor projection's struct selection should be wired to status/event IDs and stop relying on load-time tie-breaks for same-event determinism. API/schema catalog (`api/catalog.py:621`), publication key/contracts (`publication.py:101`), migration catalog fields, and latest-state quality counts (`quality/checks_identities.py:225-229`) must describe/handle revision states and distinguish invalid states from valid coverage. Rebuild daily panels and projections from the corrected canonical stream.

7. **Bound publication and resource usage.** Preserve 1 GB DuckDB/1 thread starting settings, host headroom checks and the controller's process-tree guard. One heavy workload only. Begin with small security/event blocks; row counts and local frame widths determine chunk sizes, not an unbounded issuer history assumption. Estimated frame work is proportional to the generated affected-event keys times that metric's bounded local frame width and input count; interval joins avoid rejoining every prior revision at every event. Stage outputs in SQL or bounded sorted Parquet blocks with resumable counts/checksums, build/validate primary keys incrementally under the same bounded publication discipline, and atomically activate a completed generation/scope. Do not wrap the full-universe revision expansion in one giant delete/insert/index-building transaction. A staged generation must not expose mixed writer/consumer contracts. Definition changes require dependent closure invalidation/rebuild, not only recomputing requested parents.

For a known invalidation example, take `safe_div(A, B)` with A=100 and B=10 known at t1, B amended to 0 at t2, and B amended to 20 at t3. Required states are `(10, valid, t1)`, `(NULL, zero_denominator, t2)`, `(5, valid, t3)`. An ASOF read in `[t2,t3)` must return the invalid state; filtering nulls before ranking would incorrectly resurrect 10. This follows from supported numeric zero revisions and does not depend on an upstream null-withdrawal capability.

## Focused acceptance evidence for the implementation task

No checks were executed in this audit. The implementation needs one focused verification set, under the controller's exclusive heavy-work slot: the exact amendment table above, chained gross margin/TTM and four-quarter growth impacts, invalidation/recovery, coalesce fallback clock, delayed missing-quarter arrival, an older-period amendment with a newer unaffected raw period, deterministic same-timestamp ties, and event chunk boundaries. Assert both pre- and post-amendment exports, daily PS values, IDs/lineage, and rebuild equivalence. A bounded synthetic stress case should verify frame/event row growth without any universe-wide Cartesian product. Live capacity and coverage remain operator measurements after the active writer and schema coordination permit them.

**Disposition:** confirmed Critical production PIT defect. Numeric availability timestamps are present, but complete derived availability history is not. Coordinated event-state implementation and downstream rebuild are required; no changes were made by this audit.
