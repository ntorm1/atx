Reserved migrations 0172–0175.

**Mandate / Owns:** panel-gating wiring in `db/orchestrator.py` (a new gate hook for the factor-panel dataset(s), extending pf2-S10's step-quality-gate); factor SLAs + anomaly + lineage-completeness in `db/observability.py`; the surpass-ledger advance in `db/parity.py` (`PROVIDER_PARITY_ROWS` → `seed_provider_parity_matrix`); the reproducible full-rebuild + backfill runbook (`docs/` + a thin `scripts/` driver referencing S1's `scripts/warehouse_backfill.py` and pf2-S10's `scripts/warehouse_rebuild.py`); NEW `db/tests/test_pf3_capstone.py`.

## Baseline / where the cycles go

pf3 will, by S11, have built a complete, dense, PIT-safe factor product — but nothing yet *guards* it in production. Measured 2026-07-04 against `atx-impl/db` and the pf2-S10 surfaces pf3 extends.

1. **The factor panel + signal-eval are UNGATED.** pf2-S10 defined clause (G) and wired a `severity=critical` gate into `DatasetOrchestrator` that **halts** a run on a fundamentals-quality failure (raising `OrchestratorRunError`, `record_audit(action="step_quality_gate_halt")`, downstream left `pending`). But that gate covers the *fundamentals* datasets. The **factor-panel** and **signal-eval** datasets (S7–S11) are outside it: S11 authors leakage + coverage DQC and S10 authors export-contract checks, but no orchestrator path treats a factor-panel-critical failure as a *halt*. A broken factor (leakage), a leaky operator (cross-sectional rank over a future cross-section), or a stale/empty panel could ship a green run.

2. **`observability.py` watches fundamentals, not factors.** pf2-S10's `db/observability.py` evaluates per-dataset **freshness SLAs** against `dataset_watermarks` and detects **row-count anomalies** over `data_quality_checks` history — but its SLA rows and anomaly baselines cover the fundamentals/pricing datasets. There is **no factor freshness SLA** (is the panel as-of the latest trading day?), **no panel row-count anomaly** (did today's cross-section collapse from ~4,000 names to 40?), and **no lineage-completeness** signal (is every panel factor still traceable to source facts + formula + vintage — surpass axis 1 — or did a factor silently lose its lineage edge?).

3. **The backfill DAG exists but its maintenance SCHEDULE isn't codified.** S1 builds a windowed/chunked/resumable **backfill mode** and an **incremental-maintenance mode** with per-partition watermarks. But *when* each dataset re-runs (daily price bars, quarterly fundamentals, on-filing revisions, factor-panel rebuild cadence) is not written down as data — the DAG *can* run incrementally, but the operating cadence is tribal knowledge, not a codified schedule an operator or a fresh agent can execute.

4. **No reproducible full-rebuild + backfill runbook.** pf2-S10 shipped a whole-*warehouse* rebuild + DR runbook, but pf3 added a second rail — the historical **backfill** (S1/S4) — and a whole new **factor** layer. There is no single fresh-agent document that walks: recover → migrate → backfill (operator archive) → rebuild → gate → observe → verify, for the *factor* product specifically.

5. **The surpass axes aren't EVIDENCED in the ledger.** `db/parity.py` carries `PROVIDER_PARITY_ROWS` (frozen `ProviderParityRow`: `provider`, `warehouse_domain`, `parity_status`, `limitations`, `next_gap`, …) seeded into `provider_parity_matrix` via `seed_provider_parity_matrix` (`parity.py:768`). pf3 committed to *surpassing* FactSet/Compustat on four axes (full lineage, signal-native factors, PIT-perfect vintages + freshness, open cross-vendor recon) — but the ledger does not yet carry rows that **cite the concrete surface** proving each axis. The claim is made in the ROADMAP; it is not yet evidenced in queryable data.

**Already good — do not regress:**
- **pf2-S10's gate.** The `DatasetOrchestrator` step-quality-gate hook, its `critical`→halt / `error`→partial / `warning`→record semantics, `OrchestratorRunError`, and the `etl_job_audit` trail stay exactly as pf2 shipped them. S12 *adds* a factor-panel dataset to the gated set; it does not rewrite the evaluator.
- **`observability.py` freshness/anomaly.** The per-dataset freshness-SLA evaluator and the median/MAD row-count anomaly detector stay byte-identical; S12 adds *factor* SLA rows and *panel* anomaly baselines through the same code paths.
- **`parity.py` ledger.** `ProviderParityRow`, `PROVIDER_PARITY_ROWS`, and `seed_provider_parity_matrix` stay the surface; S12 *advances* `parity_status` and appends surpass-evidence rows through them, never forks them.
- **The deterministic orchestrator.** `DatasetOrchestrator.run/.resume`, `full_rebuild`, `RetryPolicy`, `RunManifest`, and the `etl_job_runs`/`etl_job_steps`/`etl_job_audit` ledger stay as pf1-S2/pf2-S10 shipped them.

## Tasks

### S12-0 — Backfill proof + incremental-maintenance schedule

**Root cause:** S1 built a resumable backfill mode + an incremental-maintenance mode with per-partition watermarks, but *when* each dataset re-runs — and *that a full rebuild followed by an incremental cycle is deterministic and idempotent* — is neither codified nor proven end-to-end on the pf3 factor surface (baseline 3). The DAG *can* run; the operating cadence and its determinism are undocumented.

**Fix:** codify the S1 DAG's maintenance **cadence as data** — a `maintenance_schedule` surface (migration owned in S12-0's use of 0172's registry region, or folded into 0172) mapping each dataset_id to its incremental cadence (daily bars, quarterly + on-filing fundamentals, on-restatement vintages, factor-panel rebuild trigger) and its backfill-window shape, read by a thin `scripts/` driver that plans (does not execute) the operator archive. Then **prove** on a bounded slice that a full backfill + rebuild, followed immediately by an incremental-maintenance cycle, is deterministic and idempotent: the incremental re-run is a **no-op** (clause H), and the panel rows are byte-identical across the two paths. This *proves* S1's engine on the assembled pf3 surface; it does not modify the engine.

**PIT:** (H) re-running a completed window is a no-op; per-partition watermarks record progress. (D) same source + same params → same panel rows across full-rebuild and incremental paths.

**Accept:** the maintenance schedule is queryable per dataset with its cadence + backfill-window; on a fixture slice, a full backfill+rebuild then an incremental cycle produces zero net new rows and a byte-identical panel; the schedule driver *plans* the operator archive (dry-run) without touching the live DB.

### S12-1 — Factor-panel quality gated in the orchestrator *(extends pf2-S10)*

**Root cause:** pf2-S10's gate halts on *fundamentals*-critical failures, but the **factor-panel** and **signal-eval** datasets (S7–S11) are outside the gated set (baseline 1). S11's leakage/coverage DQC and S10's export-contract checks are recorded rows, not halts — a leaky or broken or empty panel can ship a green run.

**Fix:** wire S11's leakage + coverage DQC and S10's panel export-contract checks (unit/sign/scale per clause J; universe-as-of applied; zero-lookahead per clause I) into the orchestrator's existing gate as `severity=critical` gated checks for the factor-panel dataset(s) — **reusing** pf2-S10's evaluator and halt semantics, not a new one. Migration **0172** adds the panel-gate wiring metadata: a `panel_gate_config` registry mapping the factor-panel dataset_id → the set of critical checks the gate must run before committing the panel step, catalogued in the same migration. When the gate runs after the panel step's `run()`, a `critical` panel failure raises `OrchestratorRunError` (step → `failed`, run halts, `record_audit(action="panel_quality_gate_halt")`, downstream export/eval left `pending`); `error`→ run `partial`; `warning`→ record only. Gating is opt-in per run (preserving today's ungated content-sprint tests) per clause (G).

**PIT:** (B) 0172 catalogued, `panel_gate_config` seeded with its `table_catalog`/`field_catalog` rows. (G) same registry + same panel facts → same halt; the gate reads only already-materialized panel rows (no lookahead). (I)/(J) the gated checks are exactly the leakage/unit-sign checks S10/S11 authored.

**Accept:** a fixture warehouse with one planted panel-`critical` failure (a leakage-DQC breach), driven through `DatasetOrchestrator.run(gate=True)`, **halts** at the factor-panel dataset with `etl_job_runs.status='failed'`, a `panel_quality_gate_halt` audit row, and the downstream export/eval left `pending`; the same failure planted as `error` completes `partial`; as `warning` completes `succeeded`; `gate=False` reproduces today's ungated walk; a clean panel passes.

### S12-2 — Factor observability (freshness SLA + panel anomaly + lineage-completeness)

**Root cause:** pf2-S10's `db/observability.py` watches fundamentals freshness + row-count anomalies but has no *factor* freshness SLA, no *panel* row-count anomaly, and no *lineage-completeness* surface (baseline 2) — so a stale panel, a collapsed cross-section, or a factor that silently lost its lineage edge (regressing surpass axis 1) is invisible.

**Fix:** extend `db/observability.py` with three factor-domain surfaces (migration **0173**), all routing through pf2-S10's existing gated-check path so a breach can carry `severity=critical` and halt via S12-1. (1) **Factor freshness SLA** — `factor_freshness_sla` (factor/panel dataset_id → `max_lag_days`, `severity`, `enabled`); an evaluator joins each to its `dataset_watermarks` mark and emits a severity-tagged `data_quality_checks` row when the panel is staler than its SLA (is the panel as-of the latest trading day?). (2) **Panel row-count anomaly** — `panel_rowcount_anomaly`; a median/MAD baseline over the panel's per-as-of cross-section size flags a collapse (4,000 → 40 names) using pf2-S10's anomaly detector. (3) **Lineage-completeness** — `lineage_completeness_checks` asserts every emitted panel factor still resolves a full lineage chain to source fact(s) + formula + standardization rule + vintage (surpass axis 1); a factor missing a lineage edge is flagged. All three reuse the existing SLA/anomaly primitives — S12 adds *factor* rows and *panel* baselines, it does not fork the evaluators.

**PIT:** (B) 0173 catalogued, schema/index split. (C) fixtures: a factor watermark past its SLA; a panel count series `[4000,4010,3990,4000,40]` flagging the `40`; a factor with a severed lineage edge. (D) the baseline is a pure function of recorded history → deterministic z-score.

**Accept:** a stale factor watermark emits a freshness `data_quality_checks` row of the configured severity while a fresh one emits nothing; the collapsed-panel series flags the outlier with a recorded `z_score` while a stable series flags nothing; a lineage-broken factor surfaces a `lineage_completeness_checks` failure while a fully-traced panel surfaces none; all three route through the S12-1 gate.

### S12-3 — Reproducible rebuild runbook + surpass-ledger flip + whole-branch review

**Root cause:** there is no fresh-agent runbook that reproduces the *factor* product (recover → migrate → backfill → rebuild → gate → observe → verify) offline + operator-backfill (baseline 4), and the four surpass axes are claimed in the ROADMAP but not **evidenced** in `provider_parity_matrix` (baseline 5).

**Fix:** (1) A **reproducible full-rebuild + backfill runbook** — a fresh-agent document (referencing S1's `scripts/warehouse_backfill.py`, pf2-S10's `scripts/warehouse_rebuild.py`, and S12-0's maintenance schedule) that walks recover-from-`.bak` → CHECKPOINT → migrate → operator historical backfill → deterministic rebuild through the gated DAG → freshness/anomaly/lineage sweep → verify, honest about which steps are offline-deterministic (rebuild on the slice) vs operator-run (the multi-year archive). (2) The **surpass-ledger flip** — advance `parity_status` in `db/parity.py` `PROVIDER_PARITY_ROWS` on the pf3-closed axes and add rows (migration **0174** `surpass_axis_evidence` + the four `provider_parity_matrix` rows via `seed_provider_parity_matrix`) that **cite a concrete surface/check** per axis: axis 1 (full lineage) → the S12-2 lineage-completeness check + the lineage views; axis 2 (signal-native factors) → S8's revisions-momentum / standardization-delta / footnote-segment factor rows in the catalog; axis 3 (PIT-perfect vintages + freshness) → the S12-2 factor freshness SLA + pf2-S4 vintages + pf2-S8 press-release capture; axis 4 (open cross-vendor recon) → the `fact_disagreement` surface + its published agreement SLA. Migration **0175** = final indexes + a capstone catalog sweep asserting every pf3 table is catalogued + contract-covered. (3) Then the **whole-branch review** across all of pf3 (S1–S12) by the most-capable model, followed by `superpowers:finishing-a-development-branch`.

**PIT:** (B) 0174/0175 catalogued, indexes split from schema. (D) the rebuild is a pure function of source + `git_sha` + params; the surpass evidence is data, each row citing a real surface. (E) 0175's catalog sweep is the clause-(E) close-out over the whole pf3 surface.

**Accept:** the runbook exists and a fresh agent follows it once (offline rebuild deterministic on the slice; operator-backfill step honestly marked operator-run); `provider_parity_matrix` carries four rows with `parity_status` advanced to *surpassed* and each row + its `surpass_axis_evidence` citing a concrete surface/check; the 0175 catalog sweep is green (0 uncatalogued/contract-missing pf3 tables); the whole-branch review is clean and the branch is finished.

## Risks / guardrails

- **A gate halts a run it should not (false-halt flakiness).** Central risk: over-promoting a flaky panel check to `critical` stalls the whole factor pipeline. Mitigate by keeping the panel-`critical` set small, curated, and data-driven in `panel_gate_config` (retunable without a deploy), defaulting new panel checks to `error`/`warning`, and keeping gating opt-in per run (`gate=True`) so content-sprint tests are unaffected. The gate must halt on **real** criticals only — a leaky factor, a lookahead, a unit/sign violation, a collapsed panel — never on transient slice sparsity.
- **The rebuild runbook must be honest.** A fresh agent must *actually* reproduce it — offline-deterministic where claimed (the slice rebuild) and clearly operator-run where the archive backfill genuinely requires it. No step may claim reproducibility it cannot deliver; the offline/operator boundary is stated explicitly (data posture: pf3 proves determinism on a bounded slice, the operator runs the multi-year archive).
- **The surpass-ledger flip must be EVIDENCED, not asserted.** Each of the four axes must cite a **concrete** surface/check (a table, a view, a gated check, a factor-catalog row) — not prose. A `parity_status='surpassed'` row without a resolvable `surpass_axis_evidence` citation is a review-blocking defect.
- **Whole-branch review by the most-capable model.** The S1–S12 review that gates the finish-branch runs on the strongest available reviewer; a capstone signed off by a weaker pass is not acceptable.
- **Stay in 0172–0175.** Every new table/column catalogues in the same migration; schema/index split per the WAL precedent; timestamped DB+WAL backup before any live apply; strictly within the reserved range. Do not touch S7–S11 content bodies or S1's backfill engine.
