# Allocation against marked holdings — 2026-09-19

Proposed next implementation; no code changes, builds, or alternate performance run were made for this review. Checkpoint 6 remains the frozen fixed-target trial: 189 evaluation observations, total scheduled-target L1 change approximately 9.2187, and 31 of 37 changes after entry above 0.2. Those are target changes, not realized turnover. Its replay stopped on a missing held mark; an allocator cannot make that source-data failure disappear.

Add a constrained allocation policy at replay's existing rebalance seam. Keep the signal recipe fixed, carry actual TRI units and cash, and constrain dollar trades against the portfolio marked at the delayed execution observation. Preserve `replay_scheduled_targets` as the unchanged fixed-target API and give the new policy/profile a distinct provenance identity.

## Timing, state and units

At decision row `d`, freeze the preference vector, eligibility/readiness, risk parameters and their exact source window. At execution row `e=d+delay`, expose only current execution marks, held TRI units, cash and NAV to the allocation callback. The delayed policy cannot fetch alpha, universe, covariance, ADV or future rows from the full panel. A narrow borrowed state view should contain `decision_period`, `execution_period`, exact session keys, canonical instrument indices, current units/marked dollars, cash and pretrade NAV; the decision provider supplies a separately frozen snapshot. Returned weights and diagnostics are owned by replay. Borrowed spans expire when the callback returns.

For held units `q_i`, current TRI close `p_i`, and cash `C`, compute `h_i=q_i*p_i`, `N=C+sum(h_i)`, and `w_old_i=h_i/N`. Proposed weights `w_i` mean desired dollars `N*w_i`; the actual order is the difference from `h_i`, not from the previous target. Units remain TRI units, not shares. Actual traded dollars and fees must be recomputed from the units that `apply_target` will create, including floating-point conversion effects. Preserve its conservation and positive-NAV checks.

This is the existing hypothetical close-sizing simulation with delayed preferences and execution-time constraint reconciliation. It does not represent a submitted share order sized before that close was known, nor prove availability at an executable market-on-close cutoff. Freezing rows through `d` also does not establish original historical data vintages. An eventual live/order-level model needs separately timestamped decision information, submitted quantities and fills.

Mark every held asset before invoking the policy, including assets outside today's eligibility. A missing/nonpositive held mark fails with bound session/security identity; no stale carry, zero return, hidden deletion or retrospective exclusion. An unused missing mark is harmless; a proposed nonzero trade requiring it fails. A valid mark is not proof of tradability. An explicit trading halt with a valid mark means a no-trade constraint; a mandatory exit during that halt is infeasible.

## Bounded decision-time risk and objective

Use one diagonal estimate at a time, from unmasked observed TRI closes in the existing context, with an explicit decision endpoint translated back from the evaluation row. A small declared first recipe can use 63 adjacent daily returns ending at `d`, population variance and a declared positive floor, requiring all 63 price pairs to be finite/positive. This is a proposed engineering convention, not a tuned result. Do not bridge missing dates or filter the past by future eligibility. New positions lacking risk readiness are prohibited; a required exit can use any finite positive diagonal placeholder because its posttrade weight is fixed at zero. Otherwise an unsupported held risk estimate fails explicitly. Persist window endpoints, counts, floor and estimator version. A later expanding-prefix option can reuse the explicit `fit_end` seam after the same validation.

The solver universe is the canonical-order union of names eligible and ready at decision `d`, plus every nonzero holding at execution `e`. Never discard held IDs to meet a memory cap. Eligibility loss and lack of signal readiness must remain distinct from missing observations and security-type/borrow eligibility. No supplied field proves historical common-stock status or borrow availability.

Treat rank-derived books as preferences `a`, not expected returns. A suitable first objective is

`min 0.5*||w-a||^2 + lambda*w' Sigma_d w`.

Use `QpProblem` with `risk_aversion=0.5`, `q=-a`, and a `FactorModel` representing Hessian `I+2*lambda*Sigma_d`. For diagonal risk this is zero factor exposures, scalar SPD factor covariance and specific diagonal `1+2*lambda*variance_i`. For later factored risk, scale the factor block and specific diagonal without forming dense covariance; handle `lambda=0` with the identity model, not a non-SPD zero factor covariance. Pin lambda/configuration before a trial. Fee debits and hard budgets apply independently; do not label a tracking-loss penalty calibrated expected profit.

## Constraints, exits and fees

The existing turnover descriptor uses `sum(abs(w-w_old)) <= T`. Cvxportfolio uses half that quantity: its `delta=0.1` corresponds to this repository's `T=0.2`. Holding leverage and trading participation are separate constraints in the official API. ([Cvxportfolio constraints](https://www.cvxportfolio.com/en/stable/constraints.html))

Materialize hard gross, net, name and turnover constraints with **actual** `w_old`. Add checked per-name lower/upper bounds for no-hold, reduce-only and no-trade policies; a small adapter can replace the documented position-row bounds after materialization, without pretending NaN alpha enforces them. Pin this mapping in a focused fixture. No-hold coordinates must become exact zero: eliminate fixed variables and retain their known trade-cost contribution, or lift mathematically fixed values into the candidate and recheck every constraint afterward. Solver tolerance must not create a tiny ineligible holding; ordinary small holdings must never be thresholded away. Validate every numeric configuration and dimension before calling the current algebra, whose negative-value checks alone do not reject every NaN.

Declare exit priorities before implementation. Recommended bounded first behavior: explicit mandatory exits (including a profile that declares eligibility loss to require exit) are hard zero bounds; hard turnover remains hard; their conflict stops with an infeasibility record. Merely losing an alpha opinion can instead be a declared reduce-only, no-sign-flip bound and zero preference. Never silently promote statistical readiness loss to a broker recall. A later emergency policy may prioritize exits over the normal turnover budget only through a separately authorized, recorded emergency budget. `sum(abs(forced_exit_weights))` is only a lower bound: neutralizing the remaining book can require additional turnover. The full constraint set must be feasible. No post-solve clipping or silent elastic relaxation.

For trade-fee fraction `c=trade_bps/10000`, actual normalized turnover `tau`, and proposed prefee weights `w`, post-fee NAV is `N*(1-c*tau)`. Post-fee risky weights are `w/(1-c*tau)`. With hard `tau<=T`, require finite `r=1-c*T>0`, and solve with prefee gross `<=G*r` and per-name cap `<=cap*r`. Then recompute actual units, dollars, fees, net, gross and name exposures before committing the candidate. This conservative reserve can leave capital unused. For `T=0.2`, `c=0.0005`, `r=0.9999`; gross 1 before fees would exceed gross 1 afterward. Exact fee-coupled epigraph constraints can follow later.

From all cash, `T=0.2` allows at most gross 0.2 at entry, including both legs. Gross is an upper bound, never an equality; do not exempt startup or renormalize to one after solving. Without returns, costs or changing preferences, attaining gross one needs at least five such allocations; real drift/costs can alter that path. Exposure checks constrain the immediate posttrade portfolio; market moves and borrow can breach limits before the next rebalance. Record those daily breaches rather than claiming continuous compliance.

## Solver, memory and publication limits

Call `solve_with_cert`, require finite outputs/certificates and no infeasibility flags, and record primal/dual residuals with declared acceptance tolerances. A fixed-iteration failure may mean infeasibility or insufficient convergence; report that distinction honestly. Independently re-evaluate turnover, box/exit bounds, net and fee-adjusted exposures using actual dollar deltas. Prepare and validate a candidate portfolio, then commit units/cash/trades together. Failure must not publish a partially accepted rebalance or completed report. Existing pending/failure markers and exact source/artifact bindings remain required.

Factored covariance alone is not a memory bound. `ConstraintSet::materialize` allocates dense `A` of `(M+1)*M` doubles for net plus boxes. `qp_augment.hpp::build_augmented` reserves approximately `(M+1)*M` triplets even for sparse boxes, then constructs sparse matrices; `solve_augmented_form` keeps original and equilibrated forms. ADMM and optional polish use sparse KKT factors. `kkt_ldl.hpp::QuasiDefiniteLdl::factor_symbolic` allocates `Li/Lx` immediately after symbolic fill counts, with no byte-budget parameter. Do not claim these are all linear-memory operations.

First support only diagonal risk, boxes, net, gross and turnover: with the one dummy factor, primal dimension is `3M+1`, augmented rows `7M+4`, and KKT dimension `10M+5`. Check all products, integer-index conversions, dense-A/triplet reserve bytes and process-resident context/report buffers before allocation. Retain one decision snapshot and one solve at a time; no vector of all fitted models. Add a bounded symbolic-factor allocation check before `Li/Lx` allocation, covering both ADMM and polish, or use an explicitly conservative worst-case factor-storage bound that rejects oversized unions before solving. Account for both index and value arrays and scratch storage. Never shrink the union by dropping holdings. Keep the 3 GB monitored-child guard as additional measured evidence, not proof of allocator-level enforcement.

Smallest implementable step: a new replay policy overload plus one diagonal constrained-preference adapter, checked per-name boxes, solver/workspace budgets, and a new opt-in baseline profile. Reuse existing signals, axes, ledger, fees and borrow. Publish accepted allocation rows with decision/execution IDs, risk window/hash, marked pretrade weights, solver certificate, actual turnover and post-fee exposures; bind them to preferences and source context. No factor/sector/ownership/capacity claims in this first slice. Leave the frozen checkpoint-6 artifacts and legacy APIs unchanged.

## Focused acceptance cases

1. Price drift with an unchanged target produces a trade from marked dollars; prior-target turnover would incorrectly report zero.
2. All-cash two-leg entry obeys full-L1 0.2 and remains underinvested; no gross renormalization. Subsequent ramp uses the actual post-cost portfolio.
3. Perturb rows after decision `d`: frozen preferences/risk/eligibility remain identical. Execution marks may change sizing at `e`; execution-day alpha/risk may not.
4. Held-name eligibility exit remains in the union; a neutralizing exit requiring more than the budget fails. A missing held mark fails before allocation, including an intended exit.
5. A cap-bound book with nonzero fees satisfies actual post-fee gross/name limits and cash conservation; rejected candidates leave no accepted trade rows.
6. Tight solver/memory budgets fail explicitly with no dropped holdings, fallback fast path or completed report; final valuation still cannot rebalance.

The state/trade/cash formulation follows the distinction between current holdings, trading costs and posttrade constraints in [Boyd et al., Multi-Period Trading via Convex Optimization, sections 2 and 4](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/cvx_portfolio.pdf). Decision-relative rolling estimates are supported by [Cvxportfolio's forecaster contract](https://www.cvxportfolio.com/en/stable/forecasts.html). The concrete timing, fee reserve, source restrictions and implementation scope above are this review's proposed repository design, not claims that those sources validate the supplied data or strategy.
