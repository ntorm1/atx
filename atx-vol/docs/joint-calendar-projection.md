# Joint price projection

The r6 calendar repair projects calls and puts independently with Dykstra iterations. Its feasible set omits the affine call-minus-put bounds that the final certificate checks. Spending the sweep budget is not proof that the quotes conflict.

The r7 replacement represents both sides of every selected expiry in one convex quadratic program. Variables are node prices. Constraints are exact quote boxes intersected with intrinsic and carry bounds, strike convexity, end slopes, affine call-minus-put bounds, and calendar inequalities at union knots. Fixed prices are eliminated. Non-dividend slices additionally require their European call floor, discounted call slope, Legendre condition, and scaled European upper bound. Calendar comparisons outside a slice's strike domain are not extrapolated.

For slices with locked quotes or an active convexity chord, a feasible set can have no strict interior. SPY has positive-width quote boxes, but the call ask at strikes 800 and 803 and bid at 801 of expiry 20742 force an exactly flat convexity chord. A strictly interior barrier's failure is therefore not an admissible reason to reject such slices. An early independent SPY LP used loose raw-yield bounds; correcting its cash-PV convention and adding terminal floors found 15 asks below proven floors. Raising only those 15 incompatible asks to their floors makes all 33 positive-time expiries feasible, with at most 15/12,866 quote departures (99.8834% containment). Such quote recovery is a separate explicit caller policy. This solver never widens passed boxes.

The chosen method is an infeasible-start primal-dual predictor-corrector algorithm with a diagonal quadratic target-distance regularizer. With inequalities `A x >= b`, positive slacks `s` and multipliers `z`, each iteration solves a sparse positive definite Newton matrix `Q + A' diag(z/s) A`. A symbolic factorization is reused, and a single numerical factorization serves the predictor and corrector. The contract is a feasible price surface; the result is not claimed to minimize the target distance exactly. Requiring vanishing dual complementarity after a feasible boundary point is found can destroy the Newton system's conditioning without improving the financial contract. Fixed iteration limits provide deterministic work bounds. Exhaustion and numerical breakdown return `NonConverged`; only direct contradictory bounds establish `InfeasibleData`. A successful iterate must pass the existing independent slice and whole-overlap calendar certificates before any caller-owned node changes.

The predictor-corrector approach and reuse of factorization follow the primary literature: [Lustig, Marsten and Shanno, *On Implementing Mehrotra's Predictor–Corrector Interior-Point Method for Linear Programming*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0802022) and [Mehrotra, *Quadratic Convergence in a Primal-Dual Method*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.18.3.741). Numerical correctness here is established by residual and certificate checks, not inferred from an algorithm's name or convergence theorem.

The default configuration remains unchanged. Under experimental `inbox_projection`, the joint calendar wrapper uses this coupled solver; a single-slice strict barrier also falls back to it when no strict interior can be found. A material positive Phase-I infeasibility certificate still refuses immediately. All wrappers certify before committing, and retain the original fit if projection or certification fails. Full-surface scheduling and final-node re-implication remain separate integration steps; a fast isolated projection does not prove the complete cold-fit objective.

`QuoteSliceRepairConfig::terminal_floor_recovery` is a separate, default-off Box-certificate policy. Enabling it under Exact or with the unsupported dated-cash model returns an invalid-configuration error. For continuous-yield and aggregate-PV fits it supplies the actual per-node terminal prepaid value and discount factor to the solver. Every node must obey its intrinsic and terminal European lower floors. An observed ask that is below its own proven floor may have only its internal upper box edge raised, to the floor plus the existing fixed certificate allowance (`1e-8 + 1e-9 * max(spot, strike)`). Other asks and all bids retain their original boxes. Unquoted sides stay fixed. Raw input quotes are never changed, so containment scoring continues to count these departures. `QuoteSliceRepairRecord::terminal_floor_asks` and `terminal_floor_max_ask_raise` report the recovery.

The policy is retained on `QuoteSliceFit::terminal_floor_recovery` and copied into transactional surface trials. Later calendar and market repairs retain these terminal floors. The current fit archive record version cannot encode this policy. Both `FitArchiveWriter::add_fit` and `add_surface` therefore refuse such fits with `UnsupportedRecordVersion` in `FitRecords`, and roll back their writes. A caller must keep these fits in memory until a versioned archive field is implemented; clearing the flag to archive would discard the declared repair guarantee. Existing archives and default-off fits are unchanged.

Development evidence on the corrected SPY fixture (common spot, effective cash-PV carry, actual per-node terminal floors, and only the 15 individually impossible asks repaired): 33 slices and 12,866 prices, 40 Newton iterations, maximum primal residual 4.94e-11, every independent slice and all-pair calendar certificate passed, exactly 15 departures from original quotes. The complete test including JSON input and repeated certification took 471 ms under `vol-rel` on a contended, unpinned machine. This is neither a full-fit clock nor a performance qualification.
