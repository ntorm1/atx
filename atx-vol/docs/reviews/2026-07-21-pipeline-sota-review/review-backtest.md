## 1. CORRECTNESS — ranked defects

### P1-1. Decision and fill share one snapshot, at fitted-model mid — structural same-bar, mark-to-model execution
`src/backtest.cpp:1977-2008` (strategy loop steps 3-5): the engine adopts date-i's snapshot as base, **then** calls `strat.on_step(*base=date-i)`, then `execute()` fills the resulting entries at `lot.entry_price` and closes at `target_marks`/`fair_value` — all evaluated on the **same 19:55Z fitted surface the strategy just observed**. Entry price is the fitted surface's model fair value (`src/strategy.cpp:876` `lot.entry_price = sl.leg.model_price`; `src/dispersion_strategy.cpp:169` `lot.entry_price = built->entry_marks[i]`; even the "real quotes" route: `src/listed_dispersion_strategy.cpp:118-140` gates on and fills at `leg.model_mark`, with `raw_bid/raw_ask` recorded but never used as the fill). Failure scenario: any signal correlated with the surface fit's own deviation from tradeable quotes (dispersion implied-corr is exactly such a signal) books the fit error as day-0 PnL that no market participant could capture. The `listed_dispersion_reconciliation.cpp` model-vs-quote-mid NAV track is the honest mitigation, but it is diagnostic-only — quote-mid coverage gaps (`n_quote_mid_lots < n_held_lots`, `src/listed_dispersion_reconciliation.cpp:296-311`) mean the quote NAV sums a *subset* of legs while model NAV sums all, so `model_minus_quote_pnl` conflates fit error with coverage. Nothing on main fills at bid/ask or quote mid.

### P1-2. Default `UnpricedLotPolicy::ExcludeAndReport` silently truncates NAV
`include/atx/vol/backtest.hpp:283-291,319` (default) + `src/backtest.cpp:950-985`: when a held lot's surface is absent for a step, its PnL lane is *skipped* and the step total simply omits it; when the surface reappears, the missing step's PnL is never recovered — NAV permanently diverges from liquidation value, reported only as a count in `n_unpriced_lots`. There is no NAV re-anchoring. The SPY CLI overrides to `Error` (`examples/spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:483`), but `mag7_dispersion_backtest.cpp:190` runs with the truncating default. A production QIS default should be `Error`.

### P1-3. Hedge ledger trades shares at spot **0.0** when a surface is missing
`src/backtest.cpp:1823-1829`: `spot_of` returns `surface != nullptr ? S : 0.0`, and `HedgeLedger::hedge_daily` (`src/backtest.cpp:573-585`) then books `cash -= shares_to_trade * 0.0` — the hedge is flattened *for free*. Reachable whenever a uid with residual hedge shares has no surface on a date and the run is under `ExcludeAndReport` (option lots may be gone, so the step-level `Error` guard never fires: the shares ledger survives lot closes). Companion leak: the shares-PnL/financing loop `src/backtest.cpp:1956-1961` silently `continue`s on a missing surface, so held shares are unmarked for that step. Free-close-then-rebuy across a surface gap fabricates PnL. Should fail closed like roll-closes do.

### P1-4. No early-exercise / assignment simulation; hedge shares carry a 3M `q_eff` proxy instead of discrete dividends
- Expiry settlement is the only exercise event; every American lot is held to expiry and cash-settled at intrinsic (`src/backtest.cpp:828-841,1978-1991`). A short ITM call over an ex-date is never assigned; a long deep-ITM put's optimal early exercise is never taken (its forgone value is *in the mark* but never realized, so a hold-to-expiry book leaks the EEP at the end). Roll-at-7-DTE dispersion mostly dodges this; `HoldToExpiry`/`CloseAtHorizon` DSL books do not.
- Hedge-share carry: `src/backtest.cpp:1966-1973` uses `bs->q_eff_at(0.25)` — the surface's effective carry at a fixed 0.25y tenor — as the dividend accrual on hedge stock, and only when `shares_carry` is opted in (default off). No discrete dividend cash on ex-dates for the share ledger, while the option surfaces (corpus `cash_divs`, `src/opra_batch.cpp:191-199`) *do* price discrete dividends. A delta-hedged dispersion book across ex-dates on XOM/JPM/LLY has a systematic hedge-carry bias.
- No corporate-action handling anywhere in the Lot/universe path: a split in-window would change K/multiplier with no adjustment (`Lot` is immutable by design, `src/backtest.cpp:417-424`). Fine for the pinned Jan-Apr 2026 mega-cap fixture; unhandled for the 1030-name goal.

### P1-5. Point-in-time universe schedule cannot express removals
`src/dispersion_workflow.cpp:232-245` (`universe_at`): membership is "every row with `effective_date <= date`, last row per symbol wins", and rows must have `raw_weight > 0` (`:205`). There is no removal/zero-weight semantic, so a name deleted from the index remains in the basket forever (with its stale weight). Delisting = survivorship bias by construction the moment the schedule spans a reconstitution. The `as_of <= effective_date` guard (`:211`) is good PIT hygiene on the *add* side only. The golden fixture (single block, no in-window reconstitution) masks this completely.

### P2-6. 0DTE skip fix is correct-as-scoped but rests on an exporter invariant, and the alternate path is a whole-run abort
`src/listed_opra.cpp:286-306`: the skip fires only in the `definition == nullptr` branch. If the definitions file ever *does* contain a same-day-expiry row (the comment concedes definitions stamp expiry at midnight-UTC), line 308-310 (`definition->expiry_ts_ns <= valuation_ts_ns`) returns `Err` — killing the entire date's join rather than skipping the row. So: not masking (fail-closed), correctly date-gated (tests `ListedOpra.SkipsSameSessionZeroDte…`, `SameSessionSkipIsDateGatedNotRootGated`), but brittle — the invariant "the definition authority predates same-day rows" is enforced nowhere. Related inconsistency: the panel now stamps true 16:00-ET PM expiry instants (`src/opra_panel.cpp:559-598`, G1) while `ListedOptionQuote.expiry_ts_ns` comes from the definition's midnight-UTC stamp — two expiry conventions ~0.83 day apart coexist across the two routes (DTE selection uses the definition one).

### P2-7. Settlement price and time are model conventions, not settlement observations
Intrinsic at expiry uses `ss->pricing().S` — the **PCP-implied spot at the 15:55-ET snapshot** (`src/backtest.cpp:833-837,1986-1989`) — not the 16:00 official close/settlement value; pin risk in the final 5 minutes and any implied-vs-cash spot basis land in `pnl_settlement`. Additionally the exact-expiry-observation contract (`src/backtest.cpp:811-818`: `lot.expiry_ts_ns` must equal a snapshot `ts_ns` exactly, else `NotFound`) means real listed expiries (midnight-UTC or 16:00-ET stamps) can never settle against 19:55Z snapshots — hold-to-expiry on real contracts fails closed unless expiries are re-stamped to snapshot timestamps. Fail-closed is the right default, but it's a capability hole.

### P2-8. Quote-quality filters are asymmetric: crossed dropped, locked and zero-bid accepted
Loader drops crossed NBBO (`src/opra_panel.cpp:580-583`) and null/unset prices; `is_valid_listed_quote` (`src/listed_dispersion.cpp:198-201`) accepts `bid == 0` and locked (`ask == bid`) markets. A zero-bid leg yields mid = ask/2 — a fictional price — and can enter straddle selection and the recorded `raw_mid` PnL series. No staleness check on `quote_ts_ns` beyond "not in the future" (`listed_opra.cpp:336`); a 30-minute-old NBBO row passes as current. No size-based filtering despite `bid_size/ask_size` being carried.

### P2-9. Roll deferral can walk into the fail-closed expiry wall
`build_schedule_command` defers a roll when selection/coverage fails (`examples/spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:389-412`) with no deadline; the engine (`ListedDispersionStrategy`) then requires the *scheduled* roll date to be observed exactly (`src/listed_dispersion_strategy.cpp:100-103`). Similarly `DispersionStrategy` no-trade steps near expiry (correctly non-mutating, `src/dispersion_strategy.cpp:85-127`) can leave the cohort to cross expiry without an exact observation → run aborts (`NotFound`). Deterministic and loud, but a quote drought week before expiry kills the run instead of forcing a degraded roll.

### P2-10. `uid_for_symbol` is FNV-1a-32 with no collision registry
`src/universe.cpp:48-57,80-86`; stamped at corpus write `src/corpus.cpp:758`. Two symbols colliding in 32 bits (~1.2e-4 probability at 1030 names) alias surfaces. Mitigation exists but is incidental: same-date collision is rejected at `SurfaceSet::create` ("duplicate uid") at snapshot load — loud, not silent. Still, an explicit collision check at corpus write would turn a confusing load error into a nameable data error.

### Deterministic-replay claims — hold on main
Thread-count invariance rests on PortfolioPricer's serial-scatter reduction + static block partitions (`include/atx/vol/backtest.hpp:300-309`) and disjoint-slot fan-outs (`src/strategy.cpp:700-710`, `src/opra_batch.cpp:433-443`, `src/fit_scheduler.cpp:198-212`); tested (`SpyDispersionPnl.Determinism_TwoRunAndThreads`, `Mag7DispersionBacktest.DeterminismAcrossThreads`, `Strategy.BatchedStrikeResolveBitIdenticalToSerial`). The known ISA/laned-greeks tolerance drift is branch-only. Corpus `created_ts_ns` content-derived stamp (byte-reproducible builds) is also branch-only — main corpus builds remain wall-clock-stamped, i.e., not byte-reproducible run-to-run.

### PnL accounting identity — sound at stride, no independent reconciliation
NAV = Σ(pnl_total + settlement + shares + financing − cost) with exact add order (`src/backtest.cpp:2013-2020`); block-summed flow columns at `record_every_n>1` keep Σcolumn == nav (`backtest.hpp:380-389`); inception friction is booked into row-0 NAV (`:1894`). `BacktestExec.NavReconciliation`, `AttributionClosureIdentity`, `ZeroFrictionIdentity` cover it. What's missing: NAV is a *cumulative flow sum*, never reconciled against an independently repriced liquidation value (cash + book MTM + shares MTM); under P1-2/P1-3 the identity silently drifts. `validate_listed_reconciliation_backtest` (`src/listed_dispersion_reconciliation.cpp:341-364`) is a genuine cross-check for the listed route (model option PnL vs backtest, per row).

## 2. PERFORMANCE — remaining bottlenecks (this tree)

1. **The measured zero-copy win is not landed.** Every snapshot load on main reconstructs owned `PricedSurface`s (`src/backtest.cpp:1181-1220`); the 1008→44 ms borrow is on `feat/disp-hotpath`. Until the 57-commit reconciliation lands, replay pays full deserialization per date.
2. **Strategy-overload loads are always whole-board.** Subset-deserialize (`referenced_uids`) is wired only for the fixed-book overload's private cache (`src/backtest.cpp:1424-1434`); the strategy overload constructs its private cache without a subset (`:1836-1838`) because on_step names are "not known up front". For `ListedDispersionStrategy` this is false — the schedule enumerates every uid it will ever touch. One constructor call would cut per-date deserialization to the ~11 referenced names on any wide archive. Concrete, cheap, unclaimed.
3. **Corpus worker cap at ~9/16 is structural.** `append_date` fits one date at a time (`src/corpus.cpp:1075-1078`), so `run_bounded_fit_tasks` clamps `worker_count = min(budget, boards_this_date)` (`src/fit_scheduler.cpp:182`) — 11 boards/date caps at 11 workers; inner `fit_workers` is forced to 1 (`src/corpus.cpp:585-586,622-623`), so the date's tail is the serial SPY fit while 10 workers idle. Average ≈9/16 is exactly what this predicts. WS-BATCH's negative result (batching 82→15 calls, no win) is consistent: batching calls doesn't remove the per-date `append_date` boundary inside `CorpusBuildSession`. The lever is cross-date task pooling (all date×symbol boards in one fan-out, checkpointing per date afterward) or letting the last-board-standing reclaim inner `fit_workers`.
4. **`build-schedule` is serial and re-does I/O per date** (`examples/spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:353-421`): fresh `MarketSnapshot::load` per date (no cache/prefetch), a full `load_opra_daterange` parquet load per date inside the loop, and `hash_file` reads the *entire archive* per roll to fingerprint it (`:417`).
5. **Reconciliation pass holds every date's full snapshot alive simultaneously** (`examples/spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:492-510`, unbounded cache + `snapshot_owners`) — O(dates × board) resident memory; fine at 10 names, hostile at 1030.
6. **O(N) / O(N²) symbol plumbing:** `MarketSnapshot::uid_of` is a linear scan with a per-call `canonical_symbol` allocation (`src/backtest.cpp:1281-1294`), `provenance(uid)` linear (`:1269-1279`), `Clock::from_manifest` is O(dates×entries) (`:1094-1110`). Invisible at 11 names; real at 1030 names × 250 dates.
7. Engine-loop hygiene is genuinely good: retained pricers/workspaces, grow-only scratch (R-35 geometric growth `src/backtest.cpp:111-121`), settlement mark memo (L2) eliminating duplicate solves, generation-stamped hedge scratch, one FullGreeks pass shared between execute() and the recorded row (`ex->book_greeks` reuse `:2046-2048`). Remaining per-step redundancy is small: the VolTicks roll-close full 5-solve bundle kept only to preserve a test pin (documented at `:1775-1782`), and `signals()` re-running universe resolution + a full dispersion signal per recorded row when diagnostics are on (`src/dispersion_strategy.cpp:190-212`).

## 3. FEATURE GAPS vs a production options backtester

- **Intraday**: one snapshot/day, single suffix (`T19:55:00Z` in the run spec); `Clock` is one ref per date/partition. No multi-snapshot day, no intraday hedging cadence (HedgeSpec cadence is AtEntry/Daily only).
- **Fills/microstructure**: no quote-side fills, no partial fills, no order types, no participation/size-dependent impact on main (Almgren branch-only); spread model is symmetric half-spread on *model* mark or vega·vol-tick; `hedge_slippage_bps` exists but is set by no caller.
- **Margin/capital**: none. No Reg-T/portfolio-margin, no capital base — returns are $PnL against an undefined denominator (tearsheet `ann_return` is mean-of-$PnL × 252, not a rate of return).
- **Borrow**: flat scalar `borrow_rate` on aggregate short shares (`backtest.hpp:269-276`); the per-name HTB borrow-curve machinery in `curve.hpp` is unused by the ledger.
- **Early exercise/assignment, dividends**: §1 P1-4.
- **Walk-forward/sweeps**: none in-engine; every example is one config per process. No parameter-sweep or CV harness; sweeps are shell scripts by construction (process-boundary CLI design is explicit, `spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:2`).
- **Multi-strategy netting**: one `IStrategy`, one book, one cash ledger per run. No cross-strategy netting or capital allocation.
- **Risk limits in-loop**: none on main (WS-X's risk-limits are branch-only). A strategy can breach anything; only lot-identity/id-monotonicity invariants are enforced (`validate_strategy_transition`, `src/backtest.cpp:616-643`).
- **Tearsheet/attribution**: good Taylor-axis attribution closure (delta/gamma/vega/vanna/volga/theta/rho/charm/unexplained + settlement/shares/financing/cost) and vega-adjusted stats (`tearsheet.hpp:40-72`); missing per-name/per-cohort attribution, drawdown duration, exposure time series beyond 4 greeks, benchmark/factor views.
- **Generic strategy API**: better than the "hardcoded" framing suggests — `IStrategy` is 5 virtuals, and the `DeclarativeStrategy` DSL covers single/straddle/strangle × delta/moneyness/ATM strikes × greek-target sizing × 3 lifecycles × cross-leg vega constraints with zero bespoke code; dispersion and listed-dispersion are thin adapters. The real limits: the execution vocabulary is `Lot` (American option) + delta-to-zero share hedge only — no calendars-as-structures, no stock/cash legs in the DSL, `RiskReversal` still `InvalidArgument` (`src/strategy.cpp:535-536`), `snap_to_listed` rejected in the DSL (listed identity requires the separate schedule workflow).
- **Real-expiry settlement**: exact-timestamp contract makes hold-to-expiry over real calendars unusable (§1 P2-7).

Historical VaR overlay (`src/historical_projection.cpp` + `run-projected-var`) is a nice production touch: relative-template repricing across the archive timeline with VaR/ES — though it reprices *today's* book across historical surfaces (standard HS-VaR caveats, no scenario dampening/scaling).

## 4. WIRING — reachable vs dead config

- **Frictions**: `spy_dispersion_backtest` `run-backtest` (the headline artifact) constructs `RunConfig` with **no frictions at all** (`examples/spy_dispersion_backtest.cpp:482-484`) — the published listed-dispersion NAV is frictionless on main. `mag7_dispersion_backtest.cpp:192-199` and `spy_dispersion_pnl.cpp:453-456` hardcode 5 bps + $0.65 behind a boolean `--frictions`; no CLI exposes `half_spread_bps`, `vol_tick`, or `hedge_slippage_bps` as values. **`hedge_slippage_bps` is never set by any caller** — hedge shares always fill slippage-free.
- **Financing**: `FinancingConfig` (borrow_rate, finance_premium, shares_carry, initial_cash) is implemented and tested (`BacktestExec.Financing`, `ShareCarryAndCashFinancing…`) but wired by **zero** examples — engine feature unreachable from every CLI.
- **`SurfaceProvenancePolicy::RequireAdmittedRisk`**: implemented + heavily tested, used by no CLI — the qualified-corpus run trusts manifest admission instead of the in-loop gate it was built for.
- Also unreachable from CLIs: `record_every_n`, `QueryCacheBuildPolicy::ReuseOnly`, `settlement_mark_memo` (on by default, no off switch exposed), `ResolutionOptions::fast_screen_cold_confirm` (adaptive strike resolution), `HedgeSpec::Cadence::AtEntry`, `UnpricedLotPolicy` (fixed per example: Error in spy CLI, silent default in mag7).
- Parsed-but-narrow: `RunSpec.fit_workers` drives corpus build and VaR threads but not `RunConfig.price.n_threads` in `run-backtest`; `min_dte/max_dte/min_weight_coverage` act only at schedule build (correct, but worth knowing they don't constrain replay).
- Dead relative to this path: `Universe` LRU eviction (`src/universe.cpp:328-389`), the borrow/HTB curve set, `ExpiryCloseConvention` (superseded by always-on G1, retained `[[maybe_unused]]` `src/opra_panel.cpp:116`).

Test coverage judgment: engine mechanics are excellently covered (load-once, cache identity/staleness/ReuseOnly matrix, lot-identity invariants, settlement/L1/L2/L4 solve-economy gates, NAV/attribution identities, determinism). Systematically **uncovered**: corporate actions, early assignment, ex-div hedge carry, universe removal PIT, zero-bid/stale-quote selection behavior, the spot-0.0 hedge path, multi-day missing-surface NAV drift under ExcludeAndReport.

## 5. Verdict

This is an unusually disciplined replay engine — fail-closed everywhere it matters (exact-expiry settlement, strategy-transition invariants, provenance/identity-keyed snapshot cache, no-trade contracts that never mutate state), bit-deterministic across thread counts, with real accounting-identity tests and a genuinely reusable strategy interface — but it is a *model-on-model* simulator whose headline numbers are frictionless fills at its own fitted marks, and the sprint's own measurement (+247 → −64.6 under impact) proves the strategy conclusions are dominated by exactly the execution layer that is still unmerged. Top 3 risks: (1) same-snapshot decision/fill at model mid with the friction/impact machinery stranded on `feat/disp-hotpath` — every NAV produced by main's CLIs overstates capturable PnL by construction; (2) silent NAV truncation paths under the default `ExcludeAndReport` policy (excluded-lot PnL, spot-0.0 hedge fills, unmarked shares) that no test observes; (3) point-in-time universe machinery that cannot express removals plus absent corporate-action/assignment/dividend handling, which caps validity at the current 10-name, 4-month, no-reconstitution fixture just as the 1030-name generalization goal arrives. Top 3 investments: (1) land the disp-hotpath reconciliation (zero-copy + friction/impact + risk limits) and make a friction regime mandatory in every emitted artifact on main, then add a quote-side fill option (cross the recorded `raw_bid/raw_ask` instead of `model_mark`) so the listed route's real NBBO data actually prices executions, not just reconciliation; (2) close the accounting leak family — `Error`-by-default, fail-closed hedge spot lookup, discrete dividends on the share ledger, and a per-row NAV-vs-liquidation reconciliation assert; (3) fix the corpus scheduler's per-date fan-out boundary (cross-date task pool; subset-deserialize for schedule-driven strategies) which is both the measured ~9/16 utilization cap and the cheapest scaling lever for the 1030-name universe.
