## 0. Executive summary (ranked recommendations)

1. **Fund the per-expiry σ-Chebyshev boundary family as the inversion and certification kernel (H1 — supported).** The primitive already exists and shipped on a real SPY board: `andersen_lake_put_slice_sigma` with 8 σ-nodes gave 8.1× board-price throughput at a max price gap of 3.8e-5 vs cold over 51,755 strikes, 0 % cold fallback [47]. Its arithmetic matches the brief's: 12-16 Reference solves ≈ 2-4 ms per expiry (169-260 µs each [44]) and 3.2-7.1 µs per price from a boundary (premium quadrature, measured [44]). Generalising it to the prepared `AlExpiryGeometry` and using it for the Premium-inversion iterates and the certification grid removes most of the ~36k cold solves per SPY surface that remain after L2c × L2e [49]. Expected gain: the inversion bucket (84-91 % of the fit [43 L566]) drops from ~1.8 cold solves per quote to ~1.8 quadratures (≈ 10 µs) plus whatever confirm policy R2 keeps.
2. **Fund a confirm policy, not a faster confirm (H2 — supported, inferential grade).** A fit input accurate to ~1e-6 relative in price (0.03-0.5 bp of vol) sits 100-1000× inside the quote band: SPY's median IV band is 99.3 bp and the cohort's 300-1,200 bp [43 L333]; the vendor's own marks sit a median 9-23 bp from band mid [43 L337]; the vendor's stated pricing accuracy target is ~1/10 of the minimum price variation [18]. So the cold Reference confirm (260 µs, the floor the status file identifies [49]) should be band-gated: confirm with the Fast profile (69 µs, bound 1e-12·max(P,1) [47]) only where the band is tighter than k× the tier's tested bound, otherwise accept the family/Screen root.
3. **Promote `AlProfile::Fast` to the accurate confirm by PM ruling (directive 5).** Its measured bound vs Reference is 8e-13 abs / 1.9e-13 rel in price and 0.0054 bp IV max [47]; the special-function cost is the only remaining lever in a Reference solve (86 % of the time is `std::erfc` in 1,584 node evaluations [44]). 260 → 69 µs per confirm is a 3.8× cut on the floor with no accuracy loss that any band can see.
4. **Land L2g as briefed** (Screen seeds + confirm) — it is the fastest route to the first all-keys number; Screen's bound (≤ 1.2e-6 rel on SPY 23 d, 0.73 bp IV worst on dev200 [44]) is far inside every band in §5.
5. **Do not switch the smile family for speed (H3 — open in the literature).** No published head-to-head of channel-constrained per-expiry splines vs eSSVI vs arbitrage-free kernel smoothers on dense American index chains with latency exists (§6). The channel solve is 0.2-2.4 % of the fit [43 L566]; the family is an *accuracy* decision, and the decisive in-tree evidence (eSSVI leaves a 56 bp median-RMS W-shaped residual against the vendor's own curve [43 L308]) favours the spline. Fund LP/QP repair inside the channel [30][32] rather than a family change.
6. **HFT target rows (§7):** the only *published* scalar cold-solve class is ~10-26 µs (ALO ~100k prices/s/CPU [1]; Healy 39,040 options/s at RMSE 4.1e-5 USD on K = 100 puts [2]); 5.6 µs is Healy's *batched* number with one boundary shared across 10 spots [2]. The PM's ≤ 5 µs scalar price is therefore reachable only through a shared/interpolated boundary (R1) or a serve-path record (47-142 ns/contract in-tree [43 L485]), not through a faster cold solve. ≤ 25 µs American IV has no published source; it is consistent with 1.8 family quadratures + a Fast confirm (≈ 80 µs) only if the confirm is band-gated (R2). The 300-strike chain ≤ 1 ms is a serve-path number (300 × 47-142 ns ≈ 15-45 µs [43 L485]) and is unreachable with cold Greeks (950 µs per contract for accurate price + 5 Greeks [43 L534]). SPY ≤ 1 s single-thread beats every vendor cadence found (SpiderRock equity curves ~45 s, index < 10 s [19]; Vela refits ≥ 5 s [23]); ≤ 150 ms all cores has no source.

Verdicts: **H1 supported** (strongest: the in-tree P2.5 ship gate [47] + Chebyshev parametric convergence [6]); **H2 supported, inferential** (strongest: [43 L333] + [18]); **H3 open** (strongest partial: [43 L308] + [26]). Thin after extended search: firm-level HFT latencies for options (only one patent and one blog quantify them), 2024-26 "fast American" preprints that beat ALO (none found that benchmark against it), American-IV cost in solves per inversion (nothing published; the tree is the only source), and the exact ALO (l, m, n) tables (behind the journal paywall; reproduced only via Healy [2] and QuantLib's presets [3]).

## 2. Q1 — American option pricing on CPU, 2016-2026

### 2.1 Andersen-Lake-Offengenden and its tiers

**Verified.** ALO (J. Comp. Finance 20(1), 2016) is a spectral collocation scheme: Jacobi-Newton iteration for the exercise boundary, Gauss-Legendre quadrature, Chebyshev interpolation of a transformed boundary; the abstract states throughput "typically close to 100 000 option prices per second per CPU" and that Black-Scholes American prices can be computed "to ten or eleven significant digits in less than one-tenth of a second" [1]. The paper's own (l, m, n) tables sit behind the risk.net paywall and SSRN refused the fetch; I could not read them this session (Open question 1). Two independent reproductions stand in for them:

- **QuantLib's presets (primary source code, Spanderen 2022)** [3]: `fastScheme` = Legendre (l 7, m 2, n 7)-p 27, i.e. 7 Chebyshev nodes, 1 Jacobi-Newton + 1 naive fixed-point step, pricing quadrature 27; `accurateScheme` = Legendre-tanh-sinh (25, 5, 13)-eps 1e-8, i.e. 13 nodes, 1 JN + 4 FP, l 25; `highPrecisionScheme` = tanh-sinh (m 10, n 30)-eps 1e-10. The header defines l as the Gauss-Legendre order inside each fixed-point step, m the number of fixed-point steps ("first step is a partial Jacobi-Newton, the rest are naive Richardson fixed point iterations"), n the Chebyshev node count, p the final pricing quadrature order [3]. The maintainer's blog plots RMSE vs runtime over the ALO 6,000-option grid and states PDE and tree engines "can not compete", but publishes no numbers [4] (secondary).
- **Healy (2021), Table 6, 4,495 puts, K = 100** [2] (Verified; hardware and language are *not* stated in the paper):

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ALO m=5 knots, n=4 FP iters, l=11, p=21 | 4.1e-5 | 6.8e-4 | 1.6e-4 | 39,040 | 179,705 | 25.6 / 5.6 |

**Implication for atx-vol.** The tree's Reference scheme (12 nodes, l 24, 6 sweeps, p 48) is richer than Healy's m=7 row and comparable to QuantLib's `accurate` (13 nodes, 5 steps, l 25, tanh-sinh pricing) — the 169-260 µs cost [44] is consistent with the published ~80 µs class once the `std::erfc` tax (227 vs 95 cycles per node [44]) is removed, which is exactly what `AlProfile::Fast` does (69 µs). Screen (7 nodes, l 16, 5 sweeps) at 21 µs and ≤ 1.2e-6 relative on SPY 23 d [44] is at or past Healy's m=5 row (25.6 µs scalar at 1.6e-4 RRMSE) on both axes — on *this* host, unqualified. Nothing published on CPU runs a scalar cold solve below ~10 µs at Reference-class accuracy [1][2]; the published way below that is boundary sharing (Healy's batch column, 4.6×) [2].

### 2.2 Follow-ups 2020-2026 (what exists, what does not)

- **Negative / double-boundary rates:** Healy 2021 [2], Andersen-Lake 2021 (Wilmott) [36] — Verified; the tree refuses the double-continuation regime (`NotImplemented`) [47], which is correct for US equities at positive rates.
- **Time-inhomogeneous boundaries:** Andersen-Lake 2025 (arXiv 2502.00740) extends the boundary-first approach to models where boundaries "appear and disappear" [37] — Verified existence, no speed benchmark read.
- **Discrete / hybrid dividends:** Itkin 2025, integral equations + GIT, "expects a speedup of 100×" via the fast Gauss transform [38] — self-reported expectation, Inferential. The vendor prices discrete dividends with a spliced 301-step tree [18]; the tree's V&N lattice is 3.3× an AL price and 16.9× inside a root find [43 L406].
- **Chebyshev in parameter space:** Gaß-Glau-Mahlstedt-Mair 2018 (Finance & Stochastics) prove exponential convergence of Chebyshev interpolation in model/payoff parameters when the price is analytic in them (Theorem 2.1: error ≤ 4Vρ^-N/(ρ-1) for a function analytic in the Bernstein ellipse of parameter ρ) [6]; the Dynamic Chebyshev method moves model-dependent work offline [7]; tensor-train Chebyshev prices American Heston in 5 parameters [8]. Verified. Their American experiment interpolates (K, T), not σ, and bottoms out at the FD reference's 5e-3 accuracy at N = 5 [6] — it says nothing about the σ-convergence rate (see §4).
- **FD / stencil methods:** Ahmad et al. 2023 obtain 15×-500× over parallel FD baselines via FFT stencils [35] — not benchmarked against ALO; Healy's Table 6 shows TR-BDF2 FD is 8-30× slower than ALO at equal or worse error [2]. Verified.
- **Boyarchenko-Levendorskii sinh-acceleration:** their 2019-2024 line covers Lévy/barrier pricing with ~1e-15 precision "in seconds" [53]; no American-option sinh paper surfaced in two searches. **Lorig, Jäckel (American):** nothing found — Open question 2.
- **ML boundaries:** an LSTM boundary "up to ~100×" faster than ALO is claimed without an error table on the abstract page [41] — Inferential; the prior report already recommends against NN pricers in favour of certified Chebyshev proxies [41].

### 2.3 QD+ / Ju-Zhong / BAW class

**Verified.** Li (2010, Rev. Deriv. Res.) shows that accurate option prices and accurate critical prices are different problems and that his QD+-class approximations give "much more accurate critical stock prices than all the existing methods" (BAW, Barone-Adesi-Elliott, Broadie-Detemple, Bunch-Johnson, Zhu, Li 2008) [5]; it reports no timings. SpiderRock uses Ju-Zhong for futures options [18]. In-tree: the BAW seed costs 3.3-8.5 µs per solve (one Newton per node for 7-12 nodes) [44], i.e. ~0.5-0.7 µs per critical-price solve; a QD+ seed exists and underperformed BAW in A/B [42]. The prior MM report's conclusion stands: no algorithm change is needed in this class [42].

- **Published:** Healy's batch column (one boundary, 10 spots: 4.6×) [2]; tf-quant-finance ships a batched TensorFlow AL (tensors of shape `[num_options]`, defaults grid 10, Legendre 32, Kronrod 31, tolerance 1e-8) with no benchmark [34]. ## 3. Q2 — American implied-vol inversion: published approaches and cost

**Verified.**
- There is no closed form; practitioners root-find on a numerical pricer. QuantLib's `VanillaOption::impliedVolatility` defaults to `accuracy = 1e-4` (vol units), `maxEvaluations = 100`, bounds [1e-7, 4.0], and "a finite-difference method for American and Bermudan options" [14] — i.e. the open-source default is a 1 bp-of-vol tolerance on a ms-class forward map.
- The de-Americanisation method (Burkovska et al.) converts each American quote to a pseudo-European price with a CRR tree (Δt ≈ 0.0002) by bisection to tolerance 1e-5, then calibrates to the European prices [13]. It "performs very well" at low rates, low vol and short maturities and produces "uncontrollable outliers" in higher-rate environments; effects "increase with increasing volatility and increasing maturities" and are "stronger in-the-money"; 1-year ATM put relative price errors average 0.1-0.18 % with peaks 0.83-1.02 % at r = 7 % [13]. Dividends were not tested. The ledger's independent finding that Jäckel's LBR is "the wrong inversion for American premia" [43 L102] is the same point from the other side.
- Liu-Oosterlee et al. note the American inverse problem's difficulty deep ITM ("prices barely respond to volatility"), the absence of an analytic American vega, and that bisection "may converge slowly because of the stopping regions" [17]; their calibration uses ~1,060 function evaluations [17].
- For the **European leg** of a premium decomposition: Jäckel's "Let's Be Rational" reaches full double precision in two iterations in "just under one microsecond" [15]; FlashIV (Le Floc'h & Healy, May 2026) runs a fixed-cost branch-light Householder on a tail-stable erfcx/log-price residual, 137 ns vs 243 ns for a normalised Java LBR port, max relative error < 7.3e-14 over 68,551 cases [16] (the ns figures are from the prior report's read of the PDF [41]; the abstract confirms the baseline; preprint → Inferential for the exact ns).

**Not published (Open question 4):** solves-per-inversion for an American root find on a spectral pricer, warm starts across σ, or boundary reuse across strikes *inside* an inversion. The tree is the only source: Search = 2.31 counted cold seeds + ~4.6 uncounted warm `AloPricer` sweeps + 0.95 cold polishes = 918 µs per inversion on Reference [44]; a secant over the last two residuals cut residual evaluations 9.25 → 6.67 per quote on deep-ITM puts [43 L400]; Premium (BAW/Black-76 seed leg + cold confirm per iterate) = 1.80 cold solves per inversion, roots ≤ 2.4e-10 vol from the accurate root while Search sits up to 1.75e-3 off [45]; the mid's cold points seed bid/ask so 653 of 1,003 band edges need one solve [45]; the warm map is not the cold map (≤ 1.1e-6 vol) [43 L535].

**Implication for atx-vol.** Every published American IV path is a root find on a full pricer at ≥ 1e-5..1e-4 vol tolerance [13][14]; the tree is already an order of magnitude tighter (1e-7, with Newton distance ≤ 2.4e-10 to the accurate root [45]). The lever the literature does *not* give and the tree does is **solves per inversion**: 1.8 cold solves is the floor for a pricer-based confirm; replacing the iterate pricer with a per-expiry σ-boundary family (H1) turns those solves into 3-7 µs quadratures [44], and the confirm becomes the whole cost — which is why H2 matters.

## 4. Q3·H1 — "Precompute the boundary on a Chebyshev grid in σ per expiry; price any (K, σ) by interpolation + premium integral"

**Verdict: SUPPORTED** — with the in-tree ship-gate as the strongest evidence, the theory supporting the smoothness premise only partially, and five documented pitfalls.

### 4.1 Prior art
- **In-tree (Verified):** `andersen_lake_{put,call}_slice_sigma` builds "ONE σ-Chebyshev interpolant of y[] per (expiry, r, q) group" because "the *dimensionless* boundary y[] depends only on (σ, r, q, τ), NOT on strike, and is smooth in σ"; shipped default ON after clearing the P2.5 gate on a real SPY board: 8 σ-Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes, 8.1× board-price throughput, max price gap 3.8e-5 vs cold over 51,755 strikes, δ gap 3e-7, 0 % cold fallback [47]; the audit path routes reprices through it at 8 solves per (expiry, side) instead of 24 with "identical audit verdicts; residual shift ≤ 3.8e-5" [48]. `ExpiryRecord` is the same idea one level up (16 × 12 Chebyshev in (k, σ) of the forward-normalised premium plus a 12-term series of the exercise edge k*(σ), certified ≤ 3.3e-7 fwd vs cold AL) [43 L486]. `AlExpiryGeometry` already shares the sweep-invariant tables per (T, r, q, opts) [47].
- **Literature (Verified):** Chebyshev interpolation in option parameters converges exponentially when the price is analytic in them (Gaß et al. Theorem 2.1, rate ρ^-N) [6]; Glau et al. use the offline/online split for American options [7] and tensor-train Chebyshev for American Heston in 5 parameters [8]. Tzavalis & Wang (2003) approximate the early-exercise boundary by Chebyshev polynomials under stochastic volatility, exploiting "a log-linear relationship between the optimal exercise boundary and volatility" [56] (working paper). I found no paper that precomputes the Black-Scholes boundary on a σ-grid per expiry and prices a chain from it — the in-tree P2.5 lane appears to be the prior art (Open question 5).

### 4.2 The smoothness premise
- **Verified:** American prices are monotone and continuous in volatility for convex payoffs, extended to decreasing payoffs (the put) (Ekström 2004) [9]; the put boundary is strictly monotone in time and convex (Chen-Chadam 2007; Chen-Chadam-Jiang-Zheng 2008) [11]; the boundary at expiry is K·min(1, r/q) [10][50].
- **Inferential:** monotonicity of the boundary in σ follows from [9] (the exercise region {V = payoff} shrinks when V rises in σ), so for a put B(τ; σ) is non-increasing in σ. **Analyticity in σ** (what the ρ^-N rate needs) is not proven anywhere I found; the evidence is empirical — 8 nodes → 3.8e-5 [47]. If one reads an effective ρ off that measurement (4ρ^-8/(ρ-1) ≈ 4e-5 ⇒ ρ ≈ 4), the same bound gives ≈ 1e-7 at 12 nodes and ≈ 1e-9 at 16 — a crude order-of-magnitude extrapolation, not a measurement; the price is also first-order stationary in the boundary (the American value is a supremum over stopping rules, so a boundary error δb moves the price at O(δb²)), which is why the P2.5 price gap is small relative to any boundary gap (Inferential, standard envelope argument).

### 4.3 Pitfalls, each with its source
1. **r ≈ q and r < q.** The near-expiry boundary changes form across r = q: for q > r it tends "parabolically" to rK/q, for q ≤ r to K in "parabolic-logarithmic form" (Evans-Kuske-Keller 2002) [10]; the kernel's clamp xmax = K·min(1, r/q) encodes the limit [47]. At *fixed* (T, r, q) this is a constant, so a per-expiry family is unaffected; it bites only if (r, q) are ever interpolated across — do not. The regime split `classify_regime` already gates the European / Unsupported corners [47].
2. **Very short T.** The τ-dependence is singular (√τ, √(τ ln τ)) [10], which is why AL interpolates in √τ; the σ-dependence is not the issue, but the premium is tiny and the exercise region covers most of the chain. The shipped guard is `min_tau = 3/365` (whole slice cold) [47]; L2f's error table shows the short-dated floor is carried by the premium order p (p 32 → 48 took SPY 23 d from 3.6e-6 to 1.2e-6) [44], so a short-T family needs p ≥ 48, not more σ-nodes.
3. **σ → 0 and the perpetual limit.** The boundary is bounded (perpetual limit for T → ∞, intrinsic-side clamp for σ → 0), so the box [σ_lo, σ_hi] must be chosen from the chain's in-guard σ (shipped: auto box + `min_sigma = 0.01` guard) [47]; the non-monotone band of the kernel (≤ 1.16e-6·K·max(1, T)² inside its own exercise region) is a *kernel* artifact that an interpolated boundary inherits, so the L1c refusal must stay in front of any family-based inversion [43 L569][43 L570].
4. **Discrete dividends.** The boundary loses regularity across ex-dates (Jourdain-Vellekoop 2011, listing only) [12]; `ExpiryRecord` refuses cash dividends [43 L486] and the vendor prices them on a spliced tree [18]. H1 is for the continuous-carry expiries; keep the lattice path for ex-date-before-expiry names.
5. **The absolute-strike put formulation.** A put boundary is "reusable only at its own strike (alpha, xmax absolute)" and strike rescaling reproduces a fresh solve only "to ~a few ULP" [43 L574][47]; a family therefore serves puts by homogeneity to a few ULP — not bit-identically — which is acceptable under ruling (a) (accuracy = distance to the accurate root), but every hash set changes and must be re-baselined at promotion (ruling (b)).

### 4.4 Expected cost (arithmetic on in-tree measurements, Inferential)
12-16 Reference solves per (expiry, side) at 169-260 µs = 2.0-4.2 ms [44]; the family's prepared geometry removes the ~8 µs static bind per solve [44]; a price from an interpolated boundary = 3.2-7.1 µs (quadrature only) [44]. SPY graded-v1 has ~87 certification passes × ~325 nodes [46] and 29,725 inversions × 1.8 iterates [45]: ≈ 28k + 54k ≈ 82k quadratures ≈ 0.3-0.6 s, plus 2 × (#expiries) × 2-4 ms of family builds (SPY ≈ 30 expiries ⇒ 0.1-0.2 s), plus the confirm policy of §5. That is the first arithmetic in this sprint that reaches ≤ 1 s single-thread without a confirm miracle.

## 5. Q3·H2 — "An IV accurate to ≈ 1e-6 relative in price is sufficient as the FIT input; the accurate kernel certifies and serves"

**Verdict: SUPPORTED (inferential grade — no practitioner publishes an inversion tolerance; the band widths and vendor accuracy targets are what can be sourced).**

- **Bands (Verified in-tree):** SPY's median IV band is 99.3 bp, the cohort typical is 300-1,200 bp [43 L333]; in the core (|z| ≤ 1, 45,414 rows) the vendor's srVol sits a median 9.1-22.9 bp (by family) in absolute deviation from the mid of its own bidIV/askIV, with a median signed offset of +0.5 to +2.4 bp [43 L337][43 L343].
- **Vendor accuracy targets (Verified vendor docs, Inferential as to practice):** SpiderRock "targets numerical model accuracy that works out to around 1/10th of the minimum price variation" (≈ $0.001 for penny-pilot names), fits curves "within the bid-ask channel, rather than to fit the midpoints", reports error vs mid, and serves from "calibration records … computed using our high precision models" via "faster analytic techniques", switching to the exact models when inputs depart from the market [18][19]. Vela/Exegy Metro's fitter targets the average of each strike's bid and offer IV [23]. OptionMetrics uses a "multi-thousand step" CRR tree with no stated tolerance [21]; Cboe Hanweck a binomial tree with discrete dividends and implied borrow, "millions of option valuations per second", no tolerance [20]. QuantLib's open-source default is 1e-4 vol [14]; Burkovska's academic de-Americanisation uses 1e-5 on a tree [13].
- **The arithmetic (Inferential):** 1e-6 relative in price on a vega-bearing contract is 0.03-0.5 bp of vol (the brief's own conversion; L2f measured Screen's IV error at p99 0.043 bp, max 0.73 bp on dev200 and 0.028 bp max on SPY 23 d [44]). Against a 99 bp SPY band that is ≥ 135× inside the band at the worst contract and ≥ 2,000× at p99; against the tightest public index band I found — SPX 0DTE median ATM spread 0.20 index points [40] (blog), ≈ 19 bp of vol at S = 5,000, σ = 0.15, T = 1/365 (my vega conversion) — still ≥ 25×. The vendor's own marks deviate 9-23 bp from band mid [43 L337], 12-500× more than the tier's error. A fit that is robust to a 0.5 bp input perturbation is therefore not limited by a 1e-6-relative inversion anywhere I can source.
- **Where H2 does NOT hold:** (i) the kernel's non-monotone band near intrinsic — the inverse is ill-posed there at *any* accuracy (0.005-0.18 vol apart) [43 L536][43 L569] — refuse, do not confirm; (ii) deep-ITM long-dated high-vega rows where the *warm* map's 1e-5..6e-5 σ drift became > $0.005 [43 L269] — that is a map-consistency failure, not an accuracy-of-input one, and the family map (one cold-equivalent map per expiry) removes the warm/cold split; (iii) certification and serving — the vendor's $0.001 target [18] and the served-lane certificate (≤ $1.44e-6 on the 42-contract chain, $1.17e-4 on the 600 subset vs cold AL [43 L491]) do need the accurate kernel, exactly as H2 states.

**Implication for atx-vol.** The confirm per quote should be a *policy* keyed on band width: accept the family/Screen root when the tier's tested bound (7.8e-4·max(P,1), or 4.6e-6 for T ≤ 0.1 [44]) is < 1/k of the quote's half-spread in price for a k the PM sets (10 is defensible from the numbers above; the Screen bound is loose on the long-dated low-vol corner [44], so the gate will route those few rows to a Fast confirm at 69 µs). Promote Fast as the accurate confirm (bound 1e-12·max(P,1) [47], indistinguishable from Reference under any band) by ruling.

## 6. Q3·H3 — Per-expiry channel-constrained splines (SpiderRock form) vs eSSVI/SVI vs arbitrage-free kernel smoothers

**Verdict: OPEN in the literature.** No published comparison of these three families on dense American index chains with both fit error and latency exists; the pieces that do exist point the same way as the in-tree evidence.

- **eSSVI / SSVI (Verified):** Corbetta-Cohort-Laachir-Martini fit 12 SPX maturities (68-184 options each) in 1.2 s in un-parallelised Python, estimate "0.01 seconds or less" in C#, report "typical average option price error below 4 bips of the underlying value", fits "well within the Bid-Ask spread" except short-dated left wings, and give four explicit conditions for arbitrage-free interpolation [26]; Mingone's global reparametrisation makes every iterate arbitrage-free [27]; Gatheral-Jacquier's SPX fits are "almost perfect except for the shortest expiry" [33]. In a 2025 meta-learning paper's SPX protocol (fit on 100 context quotes per day, score the rest, 2019-23 out of sample), SSVI scores 268 bp RMSE / 151 bp MAE overall and 465 bp on > 12 m, SABR 154 / 68 bp, with per-day CPU runtimes SSVI 123 ms, GP 190 ms, SABR 1,211 ms (i7-12700) [29] — a sparse-context protocol, so the absolute errors overstate dense-chain fits (Inferential).
- **Non-parametric / kernel / spline smoothers (Verified existence, no latency):** Fengler's arbitrage-free natural smoothing splines under shape constraints, "simple to implement, computationally cheap" [31]; Cohen-Reisinger-Wang's LP repair with sparse adjustments inside bid/ask [32]; SANOS (Buehler-Horvath et al., 2026) fits strictly arbitrage-free smooth surfaces by a *linear program* with bid-ask spreads as linear penalties and claims "materially lower computational cost than most of the currently available implied-volatility surface fitting routines" [30] (preprint; the prior report's PDF read records "in seconds" for an SPX 0DTE-to-far-expiry fit [41]). Operator deep smoothing on ten years of SPX intraday data reports (per the prior report's read) ~0.5 % IV MAPE vs 1-2 % for SVI [28][41] — ICLR 2025, but the numbers are from a secondary read (Inferential).
- **The vendor's own choice (Verified vendor docs):** SpiderRock's srVol is a 29-coefficient cubic spline of vol/ATM-vol on standardised moneyness with flat wings, 14 piecewise-linear increments and 8 call/put sdiv strike adjustments, fitted inside the channel under convexity and smoothness, with "fitMaxPrcErr … zero for most (typically 90 %)" fits [19][41].
- **In-tree (Verified):** fitting eSSVI directly to the vendor's own srVol curve leaves a median per-smile RMS residual of 56 bp (p90 137) with a symmetric W shape whose turning points are knots of the vendor's grid — "a 3-dof backbone cannot bend shoulder and far wing independently where their 29-knot spline can"; error variance splits level 56 % / slope 13 % / shape 31 % [43 L308]. Pinning the vendor's family without projection made containment worse because butterflies were counted rather than projected and the Hft preset overfit — "my capacity argument was true and not actionable" [43 L315]. The channel solve itself prices zero American options and is 0.2-2.4 % of the fit [43 L566].

**Implication for atx-vol.** On accuracy-vs-vendor the spline family wins on the only dense-chain evidence that exists (in-tree); on latency every family is cheap relative to the inversions (≤ 0.1-0.2 s per 12-slice SSVI surface in C-class code [26]; the tree's channel solve is ~1 % of the fit [43 L566]). H3 should not be funded as a speed lane; fund the in-channel LP/QP repair [30][32] and the W3 accuracy lanes already planned. If a published benchmark is wanted, it has to be built here (Open question 6).

## 10. Caveats and confidence — read before acting

- **Hardware and qualification.** Every in-tree µs figure is unqualified (shared host, load 15-78 %); Healy's and ALO's hardware is unstated [1][2]; the QuantLib presets publish no numbers [3][4]. Rankings and orders of magnitude only.
- **The ALO tables were not read.** The (l, m, n) tiers in §2.1 come from QuantLib's source [3] and Healy's reproduction [2], not from the paper; if the PM wants the authors' own numbers, someone with journal access must read J. Comp. Finance 20(1) Tables 7-9.
- **Vendor statements are marketing** (Vola Dynamics, Hanweck, Block Scholes) or docs (SpiderRock, Vela); the SpiderRock cadence numbers are Verified as *stated*, not as measured.
- **H2's grade is inferential** because no practitioner publishes an inversion tolerance; the argument rests on band widths (in-tree) and vendor accuracy targets (docs). The SPX 0DTE spread figure is a blog [40]; my vega conversion of it is my own.
- **H1's smoothness-in-σ premise is empirical**, not a theorem; the extrapolation from 8 to 12-16 nodes is a Bernstein-ellipse heuristic, and the long-dated low-vol deep-carry corner is where Screen was worst [44] — certify there first.
- **Secondary reads carried over:** FlashIV's 137/243 ns [16], operator deep smoothing's 0.5 % vs 1-2 % [28], SANOS "in seconds" [30] are from the prior report's PDF reads [41]; the abstracts fetched here confirm the claims' existence but not the numbers.
- **Refuted / not supported this pass:** "a 2024-26 preprint beats ALO on CPU" — none found that benchmarks against it (the stencil paper [35] compares to FD baselines; Itkin's 100× is an expectation [38]); "the PM's ≤ 5 µs scalar cold price is state of the art" — no published scalar cold solve is below ~10 µs [1][2]; "SpiderRock-style splines are known to be slower than eSSVI" — no source either way, and in-tree the channel solve is ~1 % of the fit [43 L566].
- **Time sensitivity.** FlashIV (May 2026), SANOS (Jan-May 2026), VolNP (Sep 2025), Andersen-Lake 2025 are preprints.
